# Symptomatic Bradycardia

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) management of bradycardia with adverse features, based on the Resuscitation Council UK (RCUK) Adult Bradyarrhythmia algorithm (2025), with doses from UK summaries of product characteristics. Pregnancy, children and post-cardiac-surgery pacing wires are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Bradycardia is a heart rate below 50 beats/minute that is a problem only if it causes adverse features or risk of asystole. [1]
- Causes: sinus node disease, atrioventricular block, inferior myocardial infarction, beta-blocker, calcium channel blocker, digoxin, hypothermia, hypothyroidism, raised intracranial pressure and hyperkalaemia. [1]
- Slow output lowers blood pressure and cerebral perfusion and can progress to asystole. [1]
- Adverse features are shock, syncope, myocardial ischaemia and severe heart failure. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Light-headedness, syncope, fatigue, chest pain, breathlessness. [1]
### Examination signs
- Slow pulse, low blood pressure, cold peripheries, cannon A waves, signs of heart failure. [1]
### Red flags
- Adverse features: shock, syncope, ischaemia, heart failure. [1]
- Risk of asystole: recent asystole, Mobitz type II block, complete heart block with a broad QRS, pause over 3 seconds. [1]
- Overdose of a rate-slowing drug. [1]
### Precipitants
- Beta-blocker, calcium channel blocker, digoxin, ischaemia, hyperkalaemia, hypothermia, hypothyroidism, vagal stimulation. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Athlete's sinus bradycardia | Asymptomatic, fit, rate rises on exercise |
| Sick sinus syndrome | Alternating fast and slow rhythms |
| Complete heart block | Atrial and ventricular rates independent |
| Hyperkalaemia | Wide QRS, peaked T waves, potassium raised |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Airway, breathing, circulation; oxygen if saturation below 94%; monitor, intravenous (IV) access, 12-lead ECG. [1]
- Adverse features: atropine 500 micrograms IV. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Repeat atropine every 3–5 minutes up to 3 mg. [1]
- If still unsafe, give an infusion (isoprenaline or adrenaline) or other drug and start transcutaneous pacing. [1]
### Escalation
- Pacing or risk of asystole: urgent cardiology for transvenous pacing. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit to a monitored bed. [1]

| Parameter | RCUK bradyarrhythmia 2025 [1] | UK product labels [2] [3] [4] |
|---|---|---|
| Atropine | 500 micrograms IV, repeat to 3 mg | 500 micrograms every 3–5 minutes, maximum 3 mg |

| Pacing | Transcutaneous then transvenous | Not applicable |
| Alternatives | Aminophylline, dopamine, glucagon, glycopyrronium | Aminophylline 5 mg/kg at no more than 25 mg/minute, then 0.5 mg/kg/hour |
### Discharge criteria
- Not for ED discharge when treated for adverse features; observation until cause treated and pacing decision made. [1]
### Follow-up
- Cardiology for pacing assessment and medicine review. [1]
### Patient education
- Report fainting, slow pulse or dizziness; carry a list of heart medicines.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Avoid dehydration; limit alcohol.
### Team referrals
- Cardiology; toxicology for drug overdose. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Treat bradycardia stepwise and recognise risk of asystole.
### Key points
- Atropine 500 micrograms up to 3 mg first. [1]
- Pace early if risk of asystole. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Atropine in a transplanted heart does not work. [1]
- Missing hyperkalaemia as a cause. [1]
- Delaying pacing. [1]
