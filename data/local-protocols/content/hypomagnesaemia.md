# Hypomagnesaemia

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) recognition and replacement of hypomagnesaemia, based on the NHS Specialist Pharmacy Service (SPS) December 2024 article and the NHS Highland adult guideline. Critically ill patients, refeeding risk, pre-eclampsia and torsades are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Hypomagnesaemia is a serum magnesium below 0.7 mmol/L. [1]
- Causes: loop and thiazide diuretics, proton pump inhibitors, aminoglycosides, tacrolimus, alcohol, diarrhoea, malabsorption and poor intake. [1]
- Low magnesium causes low potassium and calcium that will not correct until magnesium is replaced. [1]
- Severe deficiency causes tetany, seizures and arrhythmias. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Often none; lethargy, cramps, weakness (mild). [1]
- Tetany, seizures, palpitations (severe). [1]
### Examination signs
- Nystagmus, tremor, hyperreflexia, tetany; arrhythmia on the electrocardiogram (ECG). [1]
### Red flags
- Magnesium below 0.5 mmol/L with neurological or cardiac signs. [1]
- Arrhythmia, seizure or tetany. [1]
- Renal impairment (risk of over-replacement). [1] [2]
### Precipitants
- Diuretics, proton pump inhibitors, aminoglycosides, tacrolimus, alcohol use, diarrhoea. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Hypocalcaemia | Low calcium; may coexist with low magnesium |
| Hypokalaemia | Low potassium; may coexist |
| Alcohol withdrawal | History of alcohol use; autonomic overactivity |
| Primary arrhythmia | Normal magnesium |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Severe or symptomatic: IV magnesium sulfate in a monitored setting. [1]
- Unstable with arrhythmia: bolus magnesium sulfate (NHS Highland). [2]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Repeat according to the serum level; check magnesium after each IV course. [1] [2]
- Correct potassium and calcium and review causative medicines. [1]
### Escalation
- eGFR below 30 mL/min or unstable: reduce dose, seek nephrology advice. [1] [2]
- Signs of hypermagnesaemia (respiratory depression, loss of reflexes, hypotension): stop and give 5 mmol IV calcium (NHS Highland). [2]
### Disposition
- Admit severe or symptomatic hypomagnesaemia for IV replacement. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NHS SPS Dec 2024 [1] | NHS Highland TAM292 [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Thresholds | Mild 0.5–0.7; severe below 0.5 mmol/L | 0.5–0.7 oral; below 0.5 IV |
| Oral | 10–24 mmol/day in divided doses | One 10 mmol sachet once or twice daily |
| IV dose | 5 g (20 mmol); mild 1–5 g (4–20 mmol) | 20 mmol in 250 mL glucose 5% over 3 hours, three doses 12 hours apart |
| Rate | 1–2 g/hour usual; maximum 9 g/hour | Over 3 hours (about 6.7 mmol/hour); unstable bolus 1–2 g over 2–15 minutes |
| Renal | CKD 1–3: 25–50% lower; CKD 4–5, acute kidney injury (AKI): advice first | eGFR below 30: half dose, over 6 hours |
| Monitoring | Magnesium 7 days after oral; after each IV dose | Magnesium 6 hours after last infusion; daily until stable |
### Discharge criteria
- Magnesium at or above 0.7 mmol/L, symptoms resolved, cause addressed and oral plan in place. [1]
### Follow-up
- Magnesium at 7 days after oral therapy and at 1–3 months depending on the cause. [1]
### Patient education
- Take oral magnesium with meals; report diarrhoea.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Adequate intake and reduced alcohol.
### Team referrals
- Nephrology for CKD 4–5, AKI or dialysis. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Grade hypomagnesaemia and choose oral or IV replacement with renal safeguards.
### Key points
- Never give undiluted 50% magnesium sulfate. [1]
- Reduce dose in renal impairment. [1] [2]
### Pitfalls
- Missing causative medicines (diuretics, proton pump inhibitors). [1]
- Forgetting to correct potassium and calcium. [1]
- Over-replacement in renal failure. [1] [2]
