# Status Epilepticus

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) management of convulsive status epilepticus, based on the NHS Highland guideline TAM286 "Prolonged seizure / status epilepticus in adults (secondary care)" (version 3, amended 4 March 2026). Children, non-convulsive status epilepticus, eclampsia (see Severe Pre-eclampsia and Eclampsia) and long-term epilepsy planning are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Status epilepticus is a seizure lasting 5 minutes or longer, or repeated seizures without recovery between them. [1]
- Prolonged seizures cause hypoxia, acidosis, hyperthermia and neuronal injury, and the response to benzodiazepines falls with time. [1]
- Common causes are low antiepileptic levels, alcohol, hypoglycaemia, stroke, infection, head injury and drugs. [1]
- Up to a third of cases do not respond to a first-line benzodiazepine. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Witnessed continuous or repeated tonic-clonic movements without recovery; patient cannot give history. [1]
### Examination signs
- Generalised or focal convulsions, unresponsiveness, tachycardia, hypoxia, hyperthermia; subtle twitching with coma may be non-convulsive status. [1]
### Red flags
- Seizure lasting 5 minutes or longer. [1]
- Hypoglycaemia. [1]
- Alcohol excess or poor nutrition (thiamine need). [1]
- Unstable cardiorespiratory or neurological state. [1]
### Precipitants
- Missed antiepileptic medicine, alcohol, hypoglycaemia, stroke, central nervous system infection, head trauma, electrolyte disturbance, toxins. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Psychogenic non-epileptic seizure | Eyes closed, waxing and waning movements, preserved responsiveness |
| Syncope with jerks | Brief, with rapid recovery |
| Rigors or dystonia | Conscious throughout |
| Hypoglycaemia | Low capillary glucose that corrects with glucose |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Status epilepticus | Seizure of 5 minutes or longer, or repeated seizures without recovery [1] |
| Benzodiazepine-resistant | Seizure continues 5 minutes after the second benzodiazepine dose [1] |
| Refractory | Seizures continue despite second-line drug; needs anaesthetic [1] |
| Super-refractory | Seizures continue or recur 24 hours after third-line treatment [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–5 minutes)
- Airway, oxygen, semi-prone position, intravenous (IV) access, monitoring and 12-lead ECG. [1]
- Check capillary glucose; treat hypoglycaemia; give IV thiamine 500 mg if alcohol excess or poor nutrition. [1]
### Ongoing (5–30 minutes)
- 5 minutes: first benzodiazepine; 10 minutes: second dose if still seizing. [1]
- 15 minutes: second-line loading drug; alert the on-call anaesthetist and obtain IV or intraosseous access. [1]
### Escalation
- Under 30 minutes from onset: second IV antiepileptic or phenobarbital; over 30 minutes: general anaesthesia without delay. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit all; critical care if seizures continue despite first-line drugs, unstable physiology or after second-line failure. [1]

| Parameter | NHS Highland TAM286 v3 2026 [1] | Standard UK practice (not opened) |
|---|---|---|
| First line | Lorazepam 4 mg IV, repeat once; buccal midazolam 10 mg if no IV | Lorazepam 4 mg IV, repeat once after 10 minutes |
| Second line | Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg, phenytoin 20 mg/kg or valproate 40 mg/kg | Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg (maximum 4.5 g), phenytoin 20 mg/kg or valproate 40 mg/kg |
| Third line | Propofol, thiopental, ketamine or midazolam | General anaesthesia in critical care |
### Discharge criteria
- Seizure-free with loading and maintenance plan, cause treated, neurology review arranged; not for ED discharge after status epilepticus. [1]
### Follow-up
- Neurology and epilepsy specialist nurse; send discharge letter to the epilepsy service. [1]
### Patient education
- Take antiepileptic medicine every day; keep the same brand; know seizure first-aid and driving restrictions.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Regular sleep, limit alcohol, avoid known triggers.
### Team referrals
- Neurology; critical care; epilepsy nurse. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Follow the timed first-, second- and third-line pathway for status epilepticus.
### Key points
- Check glucose and give thiamine if indicated. [1]
- Treat early; benzodiazepines at 5 minutes. [1]
- Go to anaesthesia after 30 minutes. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Under-dosing benzodiazepines. [1]
- Phenytoin without cardiac monitoring. [1]
- Valproate in women of childbearing potential without advice. [1]
