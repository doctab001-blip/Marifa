# Acute Stroke and Thrombolysis Pathway

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) assessment of suspected acute ischaemic stroke, selection for intravenous thrombolysis and immediate antithrombotic care, based on the Actilyse (alteplase) UK summary of product characteristics (updated 8 May 2026) and National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) technology appraisal TA990 (tenecteplase). Thrombectomy selection, intracerebral haemorrhage management, subarachnoid haemorrhage and transient ischaemic attack follow-up are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Ischaemic stroke is sudden loss of brain blood flow from a blocked cerebral artery, causing a core of dead tissue surrounded by salvageable penumbra. [1]
- Each minute of delay loses brain tissue, so time to thrombolysis decides benefit. [1] [2]
- Thrombolysis is only safe after imaging has excluded intracranial haemorrhage. [1] [2]
- Causes include atherothrombosis, cardioembolism (atrial fibrillation) and small vessel disease. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Sudden face droop, arm or leg weakness, speech disturbance, vision loss, imbalance, severe headache or confusion. [1]
### Examination signs
- Facial weakness, limb drift or weakness, dysphasia, visual field defect, gaze deviation, neglect. [1]
### Red flags
- Onset within 4.5 hours (thrombolysis window). [1] [2]
- Severe headache, vomiting or reduced consciousness (haemorrhage or raised pressure). [1]
- Blood pressure above 185/110 mmHg. [1]
- Anticoagulation, recent surgery or bleeding. [1]
### Precipitants
- Atrial fibrillation, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, carotid disease, recent myocardial infarction. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Intracerebral haemorrhage | Haemorrhage on computed tomography (CT) head |
| Hypoglycaemia | Low capillary glucose, resolves with glucose |
| Seizure with Todd paresis | Witnessed seizure; weakness resolves |
| Migraine with aura | Gradual spread, headache; positive symptoms |
| Subarachnoid haemorrhage | Thunderclap headache; blood on CT or lumbar puncture |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Thrombolysis window | Within 4.5 hours of last known well [1] [2] |
| Blood pressure limit | Below 185/110 mmHg before and during treatment [1] |
| Glucose limits | Between 2.8 mmol/L (50 mg/dL) and 22 mmol/L (400 mg/dL) [1] |
| Platelets | At least 100 × 10^9/L [1] |
| International normalised ratio (INR) | 1.7 or below on warfarin [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Airway, breathing and circulation; capillary glucose; blood pressure; intravenous (IV) access; pre-alert the stroke team. [1]
- Immediate non-contrast CT head; start thrombolysis as early as possible if eligible. [1]
- Keep nil by mouth until swallow is assessed. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Thrombolysis: bolus then infusion over 60 minutes with blood pressure and neurological checks. [1]
- No aspirin, heparin or platelet aggregation inhibitors for the first 24 hours after thrombolysis. [1]
### Escalation
- Large vessel occlusion: urgent thrombectomy referral. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit to a hyperacute stroke unit. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | Actilyse SmPC (April 2026) [1] | NICE TA990 [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Time window | Within 4.5 hours of last known well | Within 4.5 hours of onset |
| Agent | Alteplase 0.9 mg/kg (maximum 90 mg), 10% bolus, 90% over 60 minutes | Tenecteplase recommended; dose not given in the section read |
| Blood pressure | Contraindicated above 185/110 mmHg; treat above 180/105 mmHg during | Not stated |
| Antithrombotics | None for 24 hours after treatment | Not stated |
### Discharge criteria
- Not for ED discharge; admit to stroke unit. [1]
### Follow-up
- Stroke clinic within 6 weeks; secondary prevention review. [1]
### Patient education
- Recognise FAST symptoms (face, arms, speech, time); call the ambulance at once; take antiplatelet or anticoagulant as prescribed.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Stop smoking, control blood pressure and cholesterol, regular exercise, reduce salt.
### Team referrals
- Stroke team; speech and language therapy; physiotherapy; neuroradiology for thrombectomy. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Select patients for thrombolysis and dose alteplase correctly.
### Key points
- Haemorrhage must be excluded by imaging before thrombolysis. [1]
- Lower blood pressure below 185/110 mmHg first. [1]
- No aspirin or heparin for 24 hours after thrombolysis. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Treating stroke mimics. [1]
- Wrong dose from estimated weight. [1]
- Missing hypoglycaemia. [1]
