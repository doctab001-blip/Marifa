# Major Haemorrhage and Massive Transfusion

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) activation of the major haemorrhage protocol and massive transfusion, based on the NHS Highland Health and Social Care Partnership Major Haemorrhage Protocol (TAM566 version 3.1, reviewed 27 April 2023) and NICE NG39 (major trauma). Obstetric haemorrhage, gastrointestinal bleeding drug therapy and paediatric dosing are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Major haemorrhage is rapid blood loss causing shock, then the "lethal triad" of acidosis, hypothermia and coagulopathy. [1]
- Early coagulopathy follows tissue injury and hypoperfusion, and is worsened by crystalloid dilution. [1] [2]
- Transfusion of stored blood lowers ionised calcium (citrate binding), causing hypotension and arrhythmia. [1]
- Early red cells with plasma in a 1:1 ratio, tranexamic acid and temperature control improve survival in trauma. [2]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Collapse, thirst, breathlessness, anxiety, visible blood loss. [1]
### Examination signs
- Tachycardia, hypotension, cold clammy skin, reduced consciousness, low urine output, distended or rigid abdomen, long-bone or pelvic injury. [1]
### Red flags
- Loss above 150 mL/minute. [1]
- Loss of 50% of blood volume in under 3 hours. [1]
- Requirement for 4 units of red cells in under 4 hours. [1]
- Anticoagulant use, liver disease, pregnancy or trauma (coagulopathy risk). [1]
### Precipitants
- Trauma, gastrointestinal bleeding, ruptured aortic aneurysm, obstetric haemorrhage, surgical bleeding, anticoagulant treatment. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Tension pneumothorax or tamponade | Distended neck veins, no volume loss |
| Septic or anaphylactic shock | Warm peripheries, fever or rash |
| Cardiogenic shock (for example from acute coronary syndrome (ACS)) | Raised venous pressure, pulmonary oedema |
| Spinal shock | Low blood pressure with bradycardia and neurological level |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Call for senior help and activate the major haemorrhage protocol; two large-bore intravenous (IV) cannulae; send bloods. [1]
- Direct pressure, tourniquet for limb bleeding, pelvic binder for suspected pelvic injury. [2] Rec. 1.5.1–1.5.3
- Warm all fluids and blood; keep the patient warm. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Give blood products by protocol, tranexamic acid and calcium; recheck gas, clotting and fibrinogen every 30–60 minutes. [1]
- Restrict crystalloid; use it only if blood is unavailable. [2] Rec. 1.5.18–1.5.22
### Escalation
- Continued bleeding after 4 units of red cells: surgical or radiological haemorrhage control, critical care. [1]
### Disposition
- Theatre, interventional radiology or critical care. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NHS Highland TAM566 (2023) [1] | NICE NG39 (major trauma) [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Activation | Over 150 mL/minute, 50% volume in 3 hours, or 4 red cell units in 4 hours | Not specified |
| Plasma to red cells | 4:4 when coagulopathy risk | 1:1 |
| Tranexamic acid | 1 g over 10 minutes within 3 hours, then 1 g over 8 hours | As soon as possible; not after 3 hours unless hyperfibrinolysis |
| Fluids | Warmed; calcium chloride 10 mL | Restrictive until bleeding controlled; crystalloid only if no blood |
### Discharge criteria
- Bleeding controlled, stable haemoglobin, clotting corrected, normal ionised calcium; stepped down from critical care. [1]
### Follow-up
- Haematology and blood transfusion service review; incident record of products given. [1]
### Patient education
- Transfused patients are told what products they received and why.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Iron-rich diet and iron replacement as advised after recovery.
### Team referrals
- Surgery, interventional radiology, critical care, haematology and transfusion laboratory. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Recognise activation criteria, give products to targets and prevent hypocalcaemia and hypothermia.
### Key points
- Early plasma with red cells in coagulopathy risk. [1]
- Tranexamic acid within 3 hours, not for gastrointestinal bleeding. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Excess crystalloid. [2]
- Not checking ionised calcium. [1]
- Delaying activation. [1]
