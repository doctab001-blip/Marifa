# Neutropenic Sepsis

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) recognition and first-hour treatment of suspected neutropenic sepsis after chemotherapy, based on the Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust Neutropenic Sepsis Pathway (October 2020) and the NHS Forth Valley Neutropenic Sepsis Antibiotic Policy (September 2017). The definition and risk-score values follow National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) CG151 practice, which was not opened. Children, stem cell transplant recipients and antifungal prophylaxis are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Neutropenic sepsis is a life-threatening infection in a patient whose neutrophil count is very low, usually after chemotherapy. [1]
- Without neutrophils, infection spreads fast and fever may be the only sign. [1]
- Common organisms are Gram-negative bacilli, Staphylococcus aureus and coagulase-negative staphylococci from central lines. [1]
- It is an acute medical emergency; antibiotics within 1 hour of diagnosis reduce deaths. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Fever (38 °C or above), rigors, malaise, cough, dysuria, mouth or perianal pain; sometimes no fever but unwell. [1]
### Examination signs
- Fever, tachycardia, hypotension, confusion, line exit-site redness, mucositis, perianal tenderness. [1]
### Red flags
- National Early Warning Score 2 (NEWS2) of 7 or above. [1]
- Hypotension or lactate above 2 mmol/L. [1]
- Multiple myeloma or central line infection. [1]
- Recent piperacillin-tazobactam in the last 28 days. [2]
### Precipitants
- Chemotherapy in the last 6 weeks (nadir 7–14 days after), haematological malignancy, central venous catheter, mucositis. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Non-neutropenic sepsis | Normal neutrophil count |
| Chemotherapy-related fever without infection | Culture negative; settles without antibiotics |
| Transfusion reaction | Temporal link to blood product |
| Tumour fever | Cancer progression; neutrophils normal |
| Drug fever | Recent new drug; rash and eosinophilia |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Neutropenia | Neutrophils below 0.5 × 10^9/L, or below 1.0 × 10^9/L expected to fall below 0.5 [1] |
| Suspected neutropenic sepsis | Temperature 38 °C or above, or other signs of sepsis, in a patient on anticancer treatment [1] |
| MASCC (Multinational Association for Supportive Care in Cancer) risk | Score 21 or above low risk; 20 or below high risk [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Treat as an acute emergency; assess NEWS2, intravenous (IV) access, blood cultures and lactate. [1]
- Start IV broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour of diagnosis. [1] [2]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Fluid resuscitation per sepsis protocol; monitor hourly. [1]
- Assess MASCC risk on day 1. [1]
### Escalation
- Shock or NEWS2 7 or above: critical care and add a stat gentamicin dose; consider meropenem. [1] [2]
### Disposition
- Admit all; 48-hour review for de-escalation; 5-day review for IV-to-oral switch. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | Gloucestershire pathway (Oct 2020) [1] | Forth Valley policy (Sept 2017) [2] |
|---|---|---|
| First line | Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g four times daily | Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g every 6 hours |
| Meropenem | 1 g three times daily (high-dose methotrexate) | 1 g every 8 hours (shock or recent pip-taz) |
| Gentamicin | 5 mg/kg once daily; 3 mg/kg if over 65; stat dose if NEWS2 7 or above | As per policy |
| Allergy | Aztreonam 2 g four times daily; ciprofloxacin 400 mg three times daily | Vancomycin, gentamicin and metronidazole 500 mg every 8 hours |
| Time to antibiotics | Within 1 hour | Within 1 hour |
### Discharge criteria
- Low MASCC risk (21 or above) and meeting local early-discharge criteria after 48 hours; otherwise remain admitted. [1]
### Follow-up
- Oncology or haematology review within 48 hours of discharge; blood count. [1]
### Patient education
- Check temperature; seek help at once for fever or rigors on chemotherapy; carry the alert card.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Hand hygiene, safe food handling, avoid sick contacts, mouth care.
### Team referrals
- Haematology or oncology; microbiology; critical care. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Start empirical antibiotics within 1 hour and review at 48–72 hours.
### Key points
- Antibiotics within 1 hour; cultures first but do not delay. [1] [2]
- Review antibiotics at 48 hours. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Waiting for the neutrophil count. [1]
- Gentamicin in acute kidney injury. [1]
- Missing a central line source. [1]
