# Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) recognition, staging and first-hour management of acute kidney injury (AKI), based on the Whittington Health NHS adult AKI guideline v2.0 (2014) and the UK Renal Registry Think Kidneys AKI care bundle (2015). Renal replacement therapy decisions, transplant recipients, pregnancy and children are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Acute kidney injury is a sudden fall in kidney function, shown by a rise in serum creatinine or a fall in urine output. [1]
- Pre-renal causes (hypovolaemia, sepsis, heart failure) are the commonest and are reversible if treated early. [1]
- Renal causes include acute tubular necrosis, glomerulonephritis, interstitial nephritis and rhabdomyolysis. [1]
- Post-renal causes are urinary tract obstruction; early ultrasound finds them. [1] [2]
- Nephrotoxic medicines (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, aminoglycosides, contrast) worsen injury. [2]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Reduced urine output, thirst, vomiting, diarrhoea, breathlessness, confusion. [1]
- Flank pain or difficulty passing urine (obstruction). [1]
### Examination signs
- Dry mucous membranes, low blood pressure (BP), tachycardia (hypovolaemia). [1]
- Raised jugular venous pressure, oedema, crackles (fluid overload). [1]
- Palpable bladder (outflow obstruction). [1]
### Red flags
- Potassium 6.5 mmol/L or above, or electrocardiogram (ECG) changes. [1] [2]
- Pulmonary oedema not responding to treatment. [2]
- pH below 7.2 or severe acidosis. [1]
- Uraemic pericarditis or encephalopathy. [1]
### Precipitants
- Sepsis, dehydration, haemorrhage, nephrotoxic medicines, contrast, obstruction. [1] [2]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Chronic kidney disease (CKD) | Known stable raised creatinine; small kidneys on ultrasound |
| Urinary retention | Palpable bladder; relieved by catheter |
| Rhabdomyolysis | Creatine kinase above 1000 IU/L; dark urine |
| Glomerulonephritis | Haematuria and protein on dipstick; hypertension |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| AKI | Creatinine rise of 26 µmol/L or more in 48 hours, or 1.5 times baseline within 7 days, or urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6 hours [1] p. 4 |
| Stage 1 | Creatinine 1.5–1.9 times baseline; urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6–12 hours [1] p. 4 |
| Stage 2 | Creatinine 2.0–2.9 times baseline; urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 12 hours or more [1] p. 4 |
| Stage 3 | Creatinine 3 times baseline or above 354 µmol/L; urine output below 0.3 mL/kg/hour for 24 hours or anuria 12 hours [1] p. 4 |

| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Renal tract ultrasound | Two normal kidneys, no hydronephrosis | Hydronephrosis | Within 6 hours if pyonephrosis suspected, otherwise within 24 hours; urology if obstructed | [2] p. 6 |
| Nephrology referral | Not needed | Stage 3, no cause found, refractory hyperkalaemia or acidosis, or pulmonary oedema | Discuss for renal replacement therapy | [1] p. 9; [2] p. 7 |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Assess volume status; give a crystalloid fluid challenge if hypovolaemic and not overloaded. [1] p. 5
- Treat potassium 6.5 mmol/L or above with ECG changes immediately (see Medicines). [1] p. 8
- Treat sepsis within 1 hour if suspected. [2] p. 5
- Stop or hold nephrotoxic and renally cleared medicines. [2] p. 5
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Reassess volume after each fluid challenge; stop when euvolaemic. [1] p. 5
- Urine output monitoring hourly in stage 2–3; catheterise if needed. [2] p. 6
- Relieve obstruction by catheter or nephrostomy via urology. [1] p. 9
### Escalation
- Refractory hyperkalaemia, acidosis, fluid overload or uraemic complications: urgent nephrology for dialysis. [1] p. 9; [2] p. 7
### Disposition
- Admit all stage 2–3 AKI and any stage 1 with a known cause not yet treated; monitor creatinine daily. [2] p. 6

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | Whittington v2.0 2014 [1] | Think Kidneys bundle 2015 [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Staging | Stages 1–3 by creatinine and urine output | Same stages |
| Fluid | Crystalloid bolus then reassess | Treat volume, sepsis and obstruction |
| Imaging | Ultrasound for obstruction | Within 6 hours if pyonephrosis, else 24 hours |
| Hyperkalaemia | Bicarbonate, insulin-glucose, salbutamol | Dialysis if refractory above 6.5 |
| Medicines | Avoid nephrotoxins | Medicines review at presentation |
### Discharge criteria
- Creatinine falling or stable at 1.5 times baseline or less, passing urine, potassium below 6.0 mmol/L, cause treated and follow-up creatinine arranged. [2] p. 6
### Follow-up
- Creatinine and electrolytes within 48–72 hours and again at 3 months. [2] p. 6
### Patient education
- Drink to thirst when well; avoid anti-inflammatory painkillers; seek help if urine stops.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Avoid dehydration and heavy alcohol; limit salt and potassium-rich foods if told to.
### Team referrals
- Nephrology; urology for obstruction; pharmacy for medicines review. [1] p. 9; [2] p. 5
### Learning objectives
- Stage AKI, find and treat the cause, and recognise emergencies needing dialysis.
### Key points
- Give fluid in measured boluses and reassess. [1] p. 5
- Hyperkalaemia with ECG changes is an emergency. [1] p. 8
- Look for obstruction early. [2] p. 6
### Pitfalls
- Continuing nephrotoxic medicines. [2] p. 5
- Missing obstruction. [2] p. 6
- Repeating fluid in a patient who is already overloaded. [1] p. 5
