# Adrenal Crisis

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) recognition and emergency treatment of adrenal crisis in people with known or suspected adrenal insufficiency, based on the Worcestershire Acute Hospitals NHS Trust guideline (WAHT-END-017 version 2.1, 2025) and the Society for Endocrinology adrenal crisis information page. Paediatric doses are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Adrenal crisis is acute cortisol deficiency that cannot meet the body's stress needs, causing shock, low sodium, high potassium and low glucose. [1]
- It occurs in primary adrenal insufficiency, secondary adrenal insufficiency and after long-term steroids when doses are missed or not increased in illness.
- Precipitants in the Worcestershire guideline: other infections 25%, gastrointestinal illness 23%, peri-surgery 10% and physiological stress or pain 9%. [1]
- Treatment is immediate hydrocortisone and rapid saline; it must never wait for tests. [1] [2]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhoea, weakness, dizziness, confusion, fever.
### Examination signs
- Hypotension or shock, dehydration, hypoglycaemia, reduced consciousness; pigmentation in primary disease.
### Red flags
- Hypotension or shock in a patient on long-term steroids or with known adrenal insufficiency. [1]
- Vomiting or diarrhoea in a patient with adrenal insufficiency. [1]
- Low sodium, high potassium or low glucose. [1]
- Steroid emergency card or hydrocortisone injection kit carried. [1] [2]
### Precipitants
- Infection, gastrointestinal illness, surgery, trauma, pain, missed steroid doses. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Sepsis | Source of infection and raised markers, though adrenal crisis may coexist |
| Diabetic ketoacidosis | High glucose and ketones |
| Gastroenteritis with dehydration | Responds to fluids alone without steroid dependence |
| Hypovolaemic shock | No steroid history; no electrolyte pattern |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Give hydrocortisone 100 mg IV or IM immediately; never wait for results. [1] [2]
- Give 500 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride over 15 minutes through a large cannula. [1]
- Check capillary glucose and treat hypoglycaemia with IV glucose. [1]
- If the patient is not known to have adrenal insufficiency, take paired cortisol and ACTH before hydrocortisone only if this does not delay treatment. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- If stable and sodium is not low, rehydrate carefully with about 3–4 L over 24 hours. [1]
- Continue hydrocortisone as 200 mg over 24 hours (infusion) or 50 mg IV or IM every 6 hours. [1] [2]
- Continue fludrocortisone at the same dose if already prescribed. [1]
- Monitor blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation and glucose; find and treat the precipitant. [1]
### Escalation
- Shock not responding to the first 500 mL: senior and critical care review. [1]
- Persistent low sodium in primary disease: specialist advice on added sodium chloride. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit all adrenal crises; refer to endocrinology. [1]

| Hydrocortisone stat | 100 mg IV or IM | 100 mg IV or IM |

| Saline | 500 mL 0.9% over 15 minutes, then 3–4 L over 24 hours if stable | Rapid rehydration with 0.9% saline; no volume or rate given |
| Glucose | IV glucose if hypoglycaemic | Not addressed |

### Discharge criteria
- Clinically recovered, back on oral steroid, a steroid emergency card, injection kit with training, sick day rules and endocrinology follow-up. [1] [2]
### Follow-up
- Endocrinology outpatient follow-up; the injection kit is added to repeat prescriptions. [1]
### Patient education
- Steroid sick day rules, when to inject hydrocortisone, and always carry the steroid emergency card. [1] [2]
### Lifestyle and diet
- Never stop steroids suddenly; double or increase doses in illness as taught.
### Team referrals
- Endocrinology; GP for the injection kit. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Recognise adrenal crisis and give hydrocortisone before tests.
- Resuscitate with 500 mL saline over 15 minutes and choose a maintenance regimen.
### Key points
- Treat on suspicion; do not wait for tests. [1] [2]
- Hydrocortisone 100 mg then 200 mg over 24 hours. [1] [2]
### Pitfalls
- Delaying hydrocortisone to obtain a cortisol. [1]
- Using too little saline or forgetting glucose. [1]
- Discharging without a kit, sick day rules or follow-up. [1]
