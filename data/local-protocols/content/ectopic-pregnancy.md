# Ectopic Pregnancy

SCOPE: Adult Emergency Department (ED) recognition and initial management of ectopic pregnancy, including the unstable patient, choice between methotrexate and surgery, and anti-D prophylaxis, based on NICE NG126 (as reproduced on ectopicpregnancy.co.uk, updated 24 November 2021) and the Whittington Health "Ectopic pregnancies - management" guideline (version 4, July 2013). Pregnancy of unknown location follow-up pathways, molar pregnancy and heterotopic pregnancy are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Ectopic pregnancy is implantation outside the uterine cavity, usually in the fallopian tube, with a risk of rupture and life-threatening haemorrhage. [1]
- Tubal rupture causes intra-abdominal bleeding and shock. [1]
- Risk factors include previous ectopic pregnancy, tubal surgery, pelvic infection, intrauterine device and assisted conception. [1]
- Treatment choice depends on the serum beta human chorionic gonadotrophin (hCG) level, mass size, fetal heartbeat, pain and ability to attend follow-up. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Lower abdominal pain, vaginal bleeding, missed period, shoulder tip pain, dizziness or collapse. [1]
### Examination signs
- Abdominal or adnexal tenderness, peritonism, tachycardia, hypotension, pallor, cervical motion tenderness. [1]
### Red flags
- Pulse above 100/minute or blood pressure below 100/60 mmHg. [1]
- Collapse, tenderness or peritonism. [1]
- Shoulder tip pain with positive pregnancy test. [1]
### Precipitants
- Previous ectopic pregnancy, tubal damage, pelvic inflammatory disease, intrauterine device in situ, in vitro fertilisation. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Miscarriage | Intrauterine pregnancy or products on ultrasound |
| Ruptured ovarian cyst | Cyst with free fluid, normal hCG pattern |
| Appendicitis | Right iliac fossa pain with normal pregnancy site |
| Pelvic inflammatory disease | Fever, discharge, negative pregnancy test |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Methotrexate | hCG below 1500 IU/L, adnexal mass below 35 mm, no fetal heartbeat, no significant pain, able to attend follow-up [1] |
| Either methotrexate or surgery | hCG 1500 to below 5000 IU/L [1] |
| Surgery | hCG 5000 IU/L or above, mass 35 mm or above, fetal heartbeat, significant pain, or unable to attend follow-up [1] |
| Methotrexate safety | Neutrophils at least 1.5 × 10⁹/L, platelets at least 100 × 10⁹/L, normal creatinine and liver function [2] |
### Bedside and bloods
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urine pregnancy test and serum hCG | Negative; hCG below 5 IU/L | Positive | Pregnancy; localise | [1] |
| Full blood count, group and save (rhesus status), crossmatch if unstable | Haemoglobin 115–165 g/L | Low | Blood loss | [2] |
| Creatinine and liver function | Creatinine 45–90 µmol/L | Abnormal | Methotrexate unsafe | [2] |
| Pulse, blood pressure | Pulse 60–100; systolic above 100 | Pulse above 100 or blood pressure below 100/60 | Unstable | [1] |
### Imaging and specialist
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transvaginal ultrasound | Intrauterine pregnancy | Adnexal mass, free fluid, no intrauterine sac | Ectopic pregnancy | [1] |
| Early pregnancy or gynaecology review | Not needed | Confirmed or suspected ectopic | Urgent referral | [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Assess airway, breathing, circulation; if unstable call senior help, insert two large-bore intravenous (IV) cannulae, crossmatch blood, give fluid and alert theatre. [2]
- Send hCG, full blood count, group and crossmatch. [2]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Stable patients: transvaginal ultrasound and gynaecology review to choose surgery or methotrexate by the criteria above. [1]
### Escalation
- Rupture or haemodynamic instability: immediate surgery (salpingectomy preferred). [2]
### Disposition
- Admit unstable patients and those for surgery; selected stable patients on methotrexate with follow-up. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NICE NG126 (via ectopicpregnancy.co.uk, 2021) [1] | Whittington Health guideline (2013) [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Methotrexate criteria | hCG below 1500 IU/L, mass below 35 mm, no heartbeat, no significant pain | Neutrophils 1.5 × 10⁹/L or above and platelets 100 × 10⁹/L or above |
| Methotrexate dose | Not given | 50 mg/m² intramuscularly as a single dose |
| Surgery | hCG 5000 IU/L or above, mass 35 mm or above, heartbeat or pain | Salpingectomy preferred; senior help, crossmatch and fluids if compromised |
| Anti-D | 250 IU for rhesus-negative women having surgery | Not mentioned |
### Discharge criteria
- Stable observations, pain controlled, hCG follow-up arranged, safety-net advice given, contraception advice and methotrexate counselling if given. [1] [2]
### Follow-up
- hCG on days 4 and 7 after methotrexate (should fall by about 15%), then weekly until negative; early pregnancy unit review. [2]
### Patient education
- Return for worsening pain, dizziness or heavy bleeding; avoid alcohol, aspirin and NSAIDs after methotrexate; contraception for 3 months.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Avoid folic acid-containing supplements until the hCG has settled.
### Team referrals
- Gynaecology; early pregnancy unit; psychological support after pregnancy loss. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Identify instability, select treatment by hCG and size, and dose methotrexate by body surface area.
### Key points
- Unstable patients need immediate surgery. [2]
- Anti-D is for surgery only. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Missing ectopic pregnancy in any woman of reproductive age with pain. [1]
- Using methotrexate with a fetal heartbeat or high hCG. [1]
- Giving folic acid or NSAIDs. [2]
