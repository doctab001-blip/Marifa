# Acute Coronary Syndrome (STEMI and NSTEMI)

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) assessment and first-hour treatment of acute coronary syndrome (ACS), covering ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) and non-ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome (NSTEMI and unstable angina), based on the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) NG185 and the Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust ACS pathway (2018), with doses from the UK summaries of product characteristics. Post-cardiac-arrest care, cardiogenic shock and long-term secondary prevention are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Acute coronary syndrome is sudden reduced blood flow to the heart muscle, usually from a ruptured coronary plaque with clot. [1]
- STEMI is a complete coronary occlusion and needs urgent reperfusion. [1]
- NSTEMI and unstable angina are partial or intermittent occlusion; troponin is raised in NSTEMI. [1] [2]
- Complications: ventricular arrhythmia, heart failure, cardiogenic shock and mechanical rupture. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Central chest pain or pressure for more than 15 minutes, spreading to arm, jaw or back, with sweating, nausea or breathlessness. [1] [2]
- Diabetic, elderly and female patients may have breathlessness, fatigue or epigastric pain only. [1]
### Examination signs
- Pallor, sweating, tachycardia or bradycardia, low blood pressure (BP), third heart sound, crackles. [2]
### Red flags
- ST elevation or new left bundle branch block with ischaemic pain. [1]
- Ongoing pain despite treatment, haemodynamic instability, ventricular arrhythmia or acute heart failure. [1] [2]
- Syncope. [2]
### Precipitants
- Smoking, hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidaemia, cocaine use, severe anaemia, tachyarrhythmia. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Aortic dissection | Tearing pain to the back, unequal arm BP; do not anticoagulate before excluded |
| Pulmonary embolism | Pleuritic pain, hypoxia, raised D-dimer or clot on imaging |
| Pericarditis | Sharp pain eased by sitting forward; widespread concave ST elevation |
| Tension pneumothorax | Absent breath sounds, tracheal deviation |
| Oesophageal spasm or reflux | Relief with antacid; normal troponin and ECG |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| STEMI | Typical pain with persistent ST elevation of 1 mm or more in two contiguous limb leads, or 2 mm or more (men aged 40 or over) / 1.5 mm or more (women) in V2–V3, or new left bundle branch block [1] section Recommendations |
| NSTEMI | Typical pain, no persistent ST elevation, high-sensitivity troponin above the 99th percentile with a rise or fall [1] section Recommendations |
| Unstable angina | Typical pain at rest or crescendo, troponin normal [1] section Recommendations |
### Bedside and bloods
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) within 10 minutes | Normal | ST elevation, ST depression, T-wave inversion | STEMI: call the primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) team at once | [2] section Pathway |
| High-sensitivity troponin | Below the local 99th percentile | Raised and rising | Repeat at 3 hours or per assay protocol | [1] section Recommendations |
| Creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) | Creatinine 60–110 µmol/L | Raised | Chooses anticoagulant; creatinine above 265 µmol/L: unfractionated heparin | [1] section Recommendations |
| Full blood count, potassium, glucose | Haemoglobin 130–170 g/L (men) | Anaemia, raised glucose | Bleeding risk; treat precipitant | [2] section Pathway |
| Chest X-ray | Normal | Pulmonary oedema, widened mediastinum | Heart failure or dissection | [2] section Pathway |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- ECG within 10 minutes; continuous monitoring and defibrillator available; intravenous (IV) access. [2] section Pathway
- Aspirin 300 mg at once (see Medicines). [1] section Recommendations
- STEMI: activate primary PCI; give fibrinolysis if PCI cannot be done within 120 minutes. [1] section Recommendations
- Give oxygen only if oxygen saturation is below 94%. [1] section Recommendations
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Second antiplatelet and anticoagulant by pathway; nitrate and opioid for ongoing pain. [2] section Pathway
- Repeat ECG at 15–30 minutes while pain persists; repeat troponin. [2] section Pathway
### Escalation
- Cardiogenic shock, sustained ventricular arrhythmia or ongoing pain: urgent cardiology and critical care. [2] section Pathway
### Disposition
- Admit all ACS to a monitored cardiology bed; STEMI to the catheter laboratory. [1] section Recommendations

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NICE NG185 [1] | Gloucestershire ACS pathway 2018 [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Aspirin | 300 mg as soon as possible | 300 mg |
| P2Y12 inhibitor | Ticagrelor or clopidogrel by risk | Ticagrelor 180 mg; clopidogrel 300 mg |
| Anticoagulant NSTEMI | Fondaparinux; unfractionated heparin if creatinine above 265 | Fondaparinux 2.5 mg; enoxaparin 1 mg/kg once daily if eGFR below 20 |
| STEMI reperfusion | PCI within 12 hours and 120 minutes; else fibrinolysis | Same approach |
| Nitrate | Not specified | GTN infusion 1–10 mg/hour if systolic BP above 100 |
| Opioid | Not specified | Morphine 5–10 mg with metoclopramide 10 mg |
### Discharge criteria
- Pain free 24 hours, troponin peaked, no arrhythmia or heart failure, revascularisation plan agreed. [1] section Recommendations
### Follow-up
- Cardiology and cardiac rehabilitation within 2 weeks; statin and antiplatelet review. [1] section Recommendations
### Patient education
- Call the ambulance for chest pain lasting more than 15 minutes; take antiplatelets daily.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Stop smoking, Mediterranean-style diet, regular exercise after rehabilitation.
### Team referrals
- Cardiology; cardiac rehabilitation; smoking cessation. [1] section Recommendations
### Learning objectives
- Diagnose STEMI quickly, choose reperfusion, and dose antiplatelets and anticoagulants safely.
### Key points
- Aspirin 300 mg at once. [1] section Recommendations
- ECG in 10 minutes; STEMI needs reperfusion. [1] [2]
- Adjust anticoagulant for kidney function. [5] [7]
### Pitfalls
- Anticoagulating an unrecognised aortic dissection. [2] section Pathway
- Giving nitrate with low BP or right ventricular infarct. [9] section 4.3
- Fondaparinux in creatinine clearance below 20 mL/min. [5] section 4.3
