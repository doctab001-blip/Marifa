# Acute Exacerbation of COPD

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) management of an acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), based on the NHS Highland COPD guideline TAM367 (version 2), with antibiotic and ventilation values from standard UK practice (National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) NG114 and British Thoracic Society (BTS) guidance, not opened) and UK summaries of product characteristics where stated. Pneumothorax, pulmonary embolism and acute heart failure as the primary cause are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
An exacerbation is a sudden worsening of breathlessness, cough or sputum in a patient with COPD. [1]
- Causes: viral or bacterial infection, pollution, heart failure, pulmonary embolism and non-adherence to inhalers. [1]
- Airflow obstruction and air trapping increase the work of breathing and can cause hypercapnic respiratory failure. [1]
- Too much oxygen in a carbon dioxide retainer can worsen acidosis, so targets are 88–92%. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Increased breathlessness, increased cough, more or purulent sputum, wheeze. [1]
### Examination signs
- Tachypnoea, accessory muscle use, wheeze, cyanosis, drowsiness or flapping tremor with hypercapnia. [1]
### Red flags
- Drowsiness, confusion or flapping tremor (hypercapnia). [1]
- Respiratory acidosis (pH below 7.35) despite treatment. [1]
- Oxygen saturation below 88% on controlled oxygen. [1]
- Cyanosis or hypotension. [1]
### Precipitants
- Respiratory infection, smoking, air pollution, cardiac failure, pulmonary embolism, uncontrolled oxygen therapy. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Pneumonia | Fever and new consolidation on chest X-ray |
| Acute heart failure | Crackles, oedema and raised natriuretic peptide |
| Pneumothorax | Absent breath sounds, pleural line |
| Pulmonary embolism | Pleuritic pain, clear chest X-ray, raised D-dimer |
| Asthma | Younger age, variable symptoms |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Exacerbation | Increased breathlessness with increased sputum volume or purulence in known COPD [1] |
| Hypercapnic respiratory failure | PaCO2 above 6.5 kPa with pH below 7.35 [1] |
| NIV indication | pH below 7.35 and PaCO2 above 6.5 kPa after 1 hour of optimal medical treatment [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Controlled oxygen by Venturi mask to keep saturation 88–92% until blood gas is known. [1]
- Nebulised bronchodilators and oral steroid (see Medicines). [1]
- Blood gas, chest X-ray, ECG. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Repeat blood gas at 30–60 minutes after any oxygen change; antibiotics if sputum purulent. [1]
- Normal carbon dioxide: target 94–98%. [1]
### Escalation
- Persistent respiratory acidosis: senior review for NIV in a level 2 area; consider critical care. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit if acidotic, hypoxic, confused, unable to cope at home or with significant comorbidity. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NHS Highland TAM367 (2021) [1] | NICE and BTS standard practice (not opened) |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen target | 88–92% with Venturi until blood gas; 94–98% if carbon dioxide normal | 88–92% |
| Nebuliser | Salbutamol 5 mg and ipratropium 500 micrograms, air-driven if acidotic | Same |
| Steroid | Prednisolone 30 mg for 5 days | 30 mg for 5 days |
| Antibiotic | Refers to formulary | Amoxicillin, doxycycline or co-amoxiclav for 5 days |
| NIV | Senior review if acidotic after treatment | pH below 7.35 and PaCO2 above 6.5 kPa after 1 hour |
### Discharge criteria
- Stable on 4-hourly or less frequent bronchodilators, no acidosis, saturation at baseline, inhaler technique checked. [1]
### Follow-up
- Community respiratory team; review within 2 weeks; pulmonary rehabilitation. [1]
### Patient education
- Know the oxygen alert card; use inhalers correctly; return early when sputum changes.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Stop smoking; stay active; keep vaccinations up to date.
### Team referrals
- Respiratory team; smoking cessation; pulmonary rehabilitation. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Give controlled oxygen, treat with bronchodilator and steroid and recognise NIV need.
### Key points
- Target saturation 88–92% until the blood gas is known. [1]
- Air-driven nebulisers if acidotic. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Uncontrolled high-flow oxygen. [1]
- Missing a pneumothorax or heart failure. [1]
- Late blood gas after an oxygen change. [1]
