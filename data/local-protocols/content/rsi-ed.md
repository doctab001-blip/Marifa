# Rapid Sequence Intubation in the ED

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) rapid sequence intubation (RSI): preparation, preoxygenation, induction agent choice by haemodynamic state, neuromuscular blocker choice, rescue and post-intubation care, based on the Vanderbilt University Medical Center Surgical Intensive Care Unit Practice Management Guideline "Rapid Sequence Intubation" (revised 1 September 2025) and the current UK summaries of product characteristics for ketamine, etomidate, suxamethonium, rocuronium and sugammadex. Paediatric, obstetric and awake intubation are outside this protocol. The unanticipated difficult airway algorithm and surgical airway are covered only as rescue steps. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
RSI gives a sedative and a rapidly acting paralytic together so the airway is secured quickly in a patient at risk of aspiration. [1]
- Preoxygenation with 100% oxygen for 3–5 minutes builds an oxygen reserve so that the patient tolerates the apnoeic period without desaturation. [1]
- Induction drugs cause hypotension in shocked or hypovolaemic patients, so the drug and dose must follow haemodynamic state, and haemodynamic optimisation comes before drug administration. [1]
- Suxamethonium raises serum potassium by about 0.5 mmol/L and is dangerous in hyperkalaemia, burns, neuromuscular disease and malignant hyperthermia, so rocuronium is used in those patients. [2]
- Rocuronium at 1 mg/kg lasts about 1 hour, and sugammadex 16 mg/kg reverses it rapidly in a cannot intubate, cannot oxygenate emergency. [2] [3]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Not applicable as symptoms: patients are intubated for failure to maintain or protect the airway, failure of oxygenation or ventilation, or an anticipated clinical course. [1]
### Examination signs
- Airway obstruction, stridor, gurgling, GCS 8 or below, respiratory distress, exhaustion, hypoxia despite oxygen, hypercapnia. [1]
### Red flags
- Anticipated difficult airway (limited mouth opening, short neck, facial trauma, obesity, airway burn or swelling). [1]
- Shock index above 0.9 or systolic blood pressure below 100 mmHg before induction. [1]
- Severe hypoxia (saturation below 90% despite high-flow oxygen). [1]
- Hyperkalaemia, burns or neuromuscular disease (no suxamethonium). [2]
### Precipitants
- Cardiac arrest, severe sepsis, major trauma, status epilepticus, overdose, acute respiratory failure, intracranial haemorrhage, airway burns. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Reversible airway obstruction | Responds to jaw thrust, suction or airway adjunct |
| Hypoxia from a treatable cause | Improves with high-flow oxygen, non-invasive ventilation or drainage of pneumothorax |
| Low GCS from hypoglycaemia or opioid toxicity | Responds to glucose or naloxone |
| Seizure postictal state | Recovers within minutes to an hour without airway support |

### Diagnostic criteria

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Prepare: monitoring, two intravenous (IV) cannulae, suction, bag-valve-mask, laryngoscope with videolaryngoscope, bougie, tube sizes 7.0 and 8.0, rescue supraglottic airway, front-of-neck access kit, and draw up drugs before starting. [1]
- Optimise haemodynamics first (fluid, blood, vasopressor ready). [1] section I
- Preoxygenate 3–5 minutes with a non-rebreather mask, bag-valve-mask, non-invasive ventilation or high-flow nasal oxygen at 100%; use a nasopharyngeal or oropharyngeal airway if tolerated. [1] section V.5
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Give the induction agent, then the paralytic; intubate when paralysed (suxamethonium 45–60 seconds, rocuronium 60 seconds); confirm tube position with waveform capnography. [1] [2]
- Start post-intubation analgesia and sedation, and treat hypotension with push-dose vasopressor. [1]
### Escalation
- Failed intubation after two attempts: call for help, use a supraglottic airway, and if cannot intubate and cannot oxygenate give sugammadex after rocuronium and perform front-of-neck access. [1] [3]
### Disposition
- Critical care or operating theatre after intubation. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | VUMC SICU RSI guideline (2025) [1] | UK summaries of product characteristics (2023–2025) [2] [3] [5] |
|---|---|---|
| Etomidate induction | 0.2 mg/kg (usually 20 mg); reduce if hypovolaemic | 0.3 mg/kg; elderly 0.15–0.2 mg/kg |
| Ketamine induction | 1–2 mg/kg | 1–4.5 mg/kg, average 2 mg/kg |
| Suxamethonium | 1.5 mg/kg | 1 mg/kg usual |
| Rocuronium | 1 mg/kg | 1.0 mg/kg for rapid sequence induction |
| Sugammadex | 16 mg/kg for cannot intubate, cannot ventilate | 16 mg/kg, 3 minutes after rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg |
### Discharge criteria
- Not applicable: all intubated patients are admitted to critical care or theatre. [1]
### Follow-up
- Critical care review within 1 hour; chest X-ray, tube position check and sedation plan; document the airway course and any difficulty. [1]
### Patient education
- After extubation explain the reason, any airway difficulty and any drug allergy found (for example anaphylaxis to a neuromuscular blocker).
### Lifestyle and diet
- Not applicable acutely; swallow assessment and stop smoking advice after extubation.
### Team referrals
- Critical care; anaesthesia and airway lead; speech and language therapy after prolonged intubation. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Prepare and preoxygenate, choose induction agent by blood pressure and paralytic by contraindications, and rescue a failed airway with sugammadex.
### Key points
- Preoxygenate for 3–5 minutes at 100% oxygen. [1] section V.5
- Sedative first, then paralytic; optimise blood pressure before induction. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Giving full-dose induction drugs to a shocked patient. [1]
- Using suxamethonium in hyperkalaemia or burns. [4]
- Not confirming the tube with waveform capnography. [1]
