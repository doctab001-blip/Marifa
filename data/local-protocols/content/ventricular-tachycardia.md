# Ventricular Tachycardia and Broad-Complex Tachycardia

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) management of a fast broad-complex rhythm with a pulse (ventricular tachycardia, torsades de pointes and irregular broad-complex tachycardia), based on the Resuscitation Council UK (RCUK) Adult Tachyarrhythmia algorithm (V3, March 2026) and the American Heart Association (AHA) 2025 Tachycardia algorithm, with doses from UK summaries of product characteristics. Pulseless ventricular tachycardia is handled in the Adult Cardiac Arrest protocol. Pregnancy and implantable defibrillator shocks are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Ventricular tachycardia is a rapid rhythm from the ventricles, usually from scar, ischaemia or a channel disorder, with a broad QRS (120 ms or longer) at 120 beats/minute or more. [1]
- Any broad-complex tachycardia is treated as ventricular tachycardia until proven otherwise. [1] [2]
- Torsades de pointes is polymorphic ventricular tachycardia with a long QT interval. [1]
- Pre-excited atrial fibrillation also gives an irregular broad-complex tachycardia. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Palpitations, light-headedness, syncope, chest pain, breathlessness. [1]
### Examination signs
- Fast regular pulse (irregular in torsades or pre-excited atrial fibrillation), low blood pressure, signs of heart failure, cannon A waves. [1]
### Red flags
- Adverse features: shock, syncope, ischaemia, severe heart failure. [1] [2]
- Loss of pulse (move to the cardiac arrest protocol). [1]
- Long QT interval or recent QT-prolonging medicine. [1]
### Precipitants
- Myocardial infarction, cardiomyopathy, hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, QT-prolonging drugs, stimulant drugs. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Supraventricular tachycardia with bundle branch block | Previous ECG shows same broad QRS; responds to adenosine |
| Pre-excited atrial fibrillation | Irregular rhythm with changing QRS width |
| Hyperkalaemia with wide QRS | Sine wave pattern; potassium raised |
| Pacemaker-mediated tachycardia | Pacing spikes |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Broad-complex tachycardia | QRS 120 ms or longer, rate above 100 beats/minute [1] |
| Unstable | Shock, syncope, ischaemia or severe heart failure [1] |
| Torsades de pointes | Twisting QRS axis with prolonged QT interval [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Monitor, defibrillator pads on, oxygen if saturation below 94%, intravenous (IV) access. [1]
- Unstable: synchronised shock, up to 3 attempts, with sedation or anaesthesia if conscious. [1]
- No pulse: start cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and follow the Adult Cardiac Arrest protocol. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Stable regular broad complex: procainamide or amiodarone. [1]
- Torsades de pointes: magnesium sulfate; stop QT-prolonging medicines; avoid amiodarone. [1]
- Irregular broad complex: treat as pre-excited atrial fibrillation or polymorphic ventricular tachycardia; seek expert help. [1]
### Escalation
- Failure after three shocks or recurrent ventricular tachycardia: amiodarone 300 mg, then critical care and cardiology. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit all patients to a monitored bed. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | RCUK tachyarrhythmia V3 2026 [1] | AHA tachycardia 2025 [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Unstable | Synchronised shock up to 3 attempts; amiodarone 300 mg over 10–20 minutes | Synchronised cardioversion |
| Stable regular | Procainamide 10–15 mg/kg over 20 minutes (maximum 1 g) or amiodarone | Procainamide 20–50 mg/minute to 17 mg/kg; amiodarone 150 mg over 10 minutes |
| Torsades | Magnesium 8 mmol over 10 minutes | Magnesium sulfate |
### Discharge criteria
- Not for ED discharge; admit for monitoring and cardiology review. [1]
### Follow-up
- Cardiology and electrophysiology; implantable defibrillator assessment. [1]
### Patient education
- Report palpitations or fainting; avoid QT-prolonging medicines.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Avoid stimulants and excess alcohol; keep potassium-rich foods in the diet.
### Team referrals
- Cardiology; electrophysiology; critical care. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Treat unstable and stable broad-complex tachycardia and torsades correctly.
### Key points
- Treat all broad-complex tachycardia as ventricular tachycardia. [1]
- Do not give verapamil. [1]
- Avoid amiodarone in torsades. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Giving adenosine to a broad irregular rhythm. [1]
- Missing hypokalaemia. [1]
- Delaying shock in an unstable patient. [1]
