# Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT)

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) management of regular narrow-complex supraventricular tachycardia (SVT), based on the Resuscitation Council UK (RCUK) Adult Tachyarrhythmia algorithm (V3, March 2026) and the American Heart Association (AHA) 2025 Tachycardia algorithm, with doses from the UK and New Zealand product information. Atrial fibrillation, broad-complex tachycardia and pregnancy are outside this protocol; cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) for pulseless rhythms is in the Adult Cardiac Arrest protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Supraventricular tachycardia is a fast regular rhythm that starts above the ventricles, usually from a re-entry circuit in or around the atrioventricular node. [1]
- Rate is typically 150–250 beats/minute with a narrow QRS. [1]
- Adenosine briefly blocks the atrioventricular node and ends re-entry rhythms. [1] [3]
- Pre-excitation (Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome) changes the safe drug choice. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Sudden palpitations, light-headedness, chest discomfort, breathlessness, anxiety. [1]
### Examination signs
- Regular pulse above 150 beats/minute, normal or low blood pressure (BP), signs of heart failure if prolonged. [1]
### Red flags
- Adverse features: shock, syncope, myocardial ischaemia, severe heart failure. [1] [2]
- Pre-excitation on the electrocardiogram (ECG). [1]
- Irregular rhythm or broad QRS (not SVT). [1]
### Precipitants
- Caffeine, alcohol, stimulants, thyroid disease, infection, electrolyte disturbance. [2]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Sinus tachycardia | Rate below 150, P waves present, gradual onset |
| Atrial flutter with 2:1 block | Flutter waves; rate fixed near 150 |
| Atrial fibrillation | Irregularly irregular |
| Ventricular tachycardia | Broad QRS, atrioventricular dissociation |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Narrow-complex tachycardia | QRS below 120 ms with rate above 150 beats/minute and regular rhythm [1] |
| Unstable | Shock, syncope, ischaemia or severe heart failure [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Oxygen if saturation below 94%, intravenous (IV) access, monitor and defibrillator, 12-lead ECG. [1]
- Unstable: synchronised cardioversion up to 3 attempts, with sedation if conscious. [1]
- Stable: vagal manoeuvre (modified Valsalva: strain for 15 seconds, then lie flat and raise legs). [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- If vagal manoeuvre fails: adenosine 6 mg, then 12 mg, then 18 mg (no pre-excitation). [1]
- If adenosine fails or is contraindicated: verapamil or a beta-blocker, then synchronised shock. [1]
### Escalation
- Pre-excitation with an irregular rhythm, or failure of three shocks: urgent cardiology and consider amiodarone. [1]
### Disposition
- Discharge after sustained sinus rhythm, no adverse features and cardiology follow-up; admit if drugs needed beyond adenosine. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | RCUK tachyarrhythmia V3 2026 [1] | AHA tachycardia 2025 [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Unstable | Synchronised shock, up to 3 attempts, sedation | Synchronised cardioversion |
| Vagal manoeuvres | First step if stable | First step if stable |
| Adenosine | 6, 12 then 18 mg | 6 mg, then 12 mg |
| Second line | Verapamil or beta-blocker, then shock | Beta-blocker or calcium channel blocker |
### Discharge criteria
- Sinus rhythm for at least 1 hour, normal vital signs, cause treated and an outpatient referral arranged. [1]
### Follow-up
- Cardiology or electrophysiology clinic for recurrent episodes. [1]
### Patient education
- Teach the modified Valsalva; return for episodes lasting over 30 minutes, fainting or chest pain.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Limit caffeine, alcohol and energy drinks.
### Team referrals
- Cardiology and electrophysiology. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Treat stable and unstable SVT with correct adenosine technique.
### Key points
- Adenosine must be a fast bolus with a flush. [3]
- Do not use adenosine or verapamil if pre-excitation with atrial fibrillation is possible. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Giving verapamil to a broad-complex tachycardia. [4] section 4.3
- Slow adenosine injection. [3]
- Missing asthma. [3]
