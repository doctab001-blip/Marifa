# Hypercalcaemia (Acute, Including Malignancy-Associated)

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) and acute medical treatment of acute hypercalcaemia, including malignancy-associated hypercalcaemia, based on the Society for Endocrinology 2016 emergency guidance and a North Glasgow tumour-induced hypercalcaemia pharmacy protocol. Chronic primary hyperparathyroidism and paediatric care are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Hypercalcaemia is a raised albumin-adjusted calcium. The commonest causes are primary hyperparathyroidism and malignancy. [1]
- Malignancy raises calcium through bone breakdown, parathyroid hormone-related peptide and, in lymphoma, active vitamin D.
- High calcium causes polyuria and dehydration, which reduces calcium excretion and worsens the rise. [1]
- Very high calcium causes dysrhythmia and coma. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Thirst, polyuria, nausea, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain, fatigue, confusion.
### Examination signs
- Dehydration, reduced consciousness, shortened QT on the electrocardiogram (ECG).
### Red flags
- Corrected calcium above 3.5 mmol/L (risk of dysrhythmia and coma). [1]
- Symptomatic hypercalcaemia of 3.0–3.5 mmol/L. [1]
- Renal failure or fluid overload risk. [1]
- Known cancer, especially lung, breast, myeloma or lymphoma.
### Precipitants
- Malignancy, primary hyperparathyroidism, vitamin D excess, granulomatous disease (for example sarcoidosis), thiazides, lithium, immobilisation.
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Primary hyperparathyroidism | High or inappropriately normal parathyroid hormone |
| Malignancy-associated hypercalcaemia | Suppressed parathyroid hormone with known or suspected cancer |
| Vitamin D toxicity or granulomatous disease | High vitamin D metabolites; steroid-responsive [1] |
| Pseudohypercalcaemia | Normal ionised calcium; high albumin or sampling error |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Confirm albumin-adjusted calcium and assess volume status. [1]
- Start IV 0.9% saline for rehydration. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Continue saline 4–6 L over 24 hours; monitor for fluid overload in elderly or renal impairment. [1]
- Loop diuretics only if fluid overload develops; they do not lower calcium. [1]
- If calcium stays high after saline, give a bisphosphonate. [1]
### Escalation
- Severe renal failure: consider dialysis. [1]
- Poor response: second-line therapy under specialist supervision. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit for IV treatment; calcium nadir after bisphosphonate is at 2–4 days. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | Society for Endocrinology 2016 [1] | North Glasgow tumour-induced hypercalcaemia protocol [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Fluids | 0.9% saline 4–6 L over 24 hours | Hydrate for 12–24 hours; no volume stated |
| Zoledronic acid | 4 mg over 15 minutes | 4 mg in 100 mL sodium chloride 0.9% over 15 minutes; 2 mg if calcium up to 3.0 mmol/L |
| Pamidronate | 30–90 mg at 20 mg/hour | 15–90 mg at 1 mg/minute by calcium level |
| Renal criteria | Slower infusion and consider dose reduction | Zoledronic acid: creatinine below 400 µmol/L, eGFR above 30 mL/min; pamidronate: eGFR above 40 mL/min |
| Recheck | Calcium nadir at 2–4 days | Urea and electrolytes and corrected calcium daily |
### Discharge criteria
- Corrected calcium falling to a safe level, drinking well, cause identified and a follow-up plan in place. [1]
### Follow-up
- Calcium recheck after the nadir (2–4 days) and specialist follow-up for the cause. [1]
### Patient education
- Drink plenty of fluids; report thirst, confusion or vomiting early.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Avoid calcium and vitamin D supplements unless advised; keep mobile.
### Team referrals
- Endocrinology or oncology for persistent or malignant hypercalcaemia; renal team for dialysis. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Grade hypercalcaemia and start saline early.
- Choose a bisphosphonate with renal checks.
### Key points
- Rehydrate first with 4–6 L saline over 24 hours. [1]
- Check renal function before a bisphosphonate. [2]
### Pitfalls
- Using loop diuretics to lower calcium. [1]
- Missing fluid overload in renal failure or old age. [1]
- Forgetting that the calcium nadir is at 2–4 days. [1]
