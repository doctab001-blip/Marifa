# Atrial Fibrillation with Rapid Ventricular Response

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) management of atrial fibrillation (AF) with a fast ventricular rate, based on the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) NG196 algorithms (April 2021) and the Resuscitation Council UK (RCUK) Adult Tachyarrhythmia algorithm (V3, March 2026), with doses from UK summaries of product characteristics. Pregnancy, Wolff-Parkinson-White pre-excitation and long-term stroke prevention planning are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Atrial fibrillation is chaotic atrial activity with an irregularly irregular ventricular rhythm; a rapid response means a ventricular rate above 110 beats/minute. [1] [2]
- Loss of atrial contraction lowers cardiac output and causes clot formation in the left atrial appendage (stroke risk). [1]
- A fast ventricular rate shortens filling time and can cause tachycardia-induced heart failure. [1]
- Triggers include sepsis, thyrotoxicosis, alcohol, electrolyte loss, heart failure and ischaemia. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Palpitations, breathlessness, light-headedness, chest discomfort, fatigue. [1]
### Examination signs
- Irregularly irregular pulse, rate often 110–180 beats/minute, pulse deficit, signs of heart failure. [1]
### Red flags
- Adverse features: shock, syncope, ischaemia, severe heart failure. [2]
- Pre-excitation (delta wave) on the electrocardiogram (ECG). [2]
- Onset of weakness or speech disturbance (embolic stroke). [1]
### Precipitants
- Infection, thyroid disease, alcohol, surgery, electrolyte disturbance, pulmonary embolism, stimulants. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Atrial flutter | Regular sawtooth flutter waves |
| Multifocal atrial tachycardia | At least three P-wave shapes; severe lung disease |
| Sinus tachycardia | Visible P waves before each QRS |
| Pre-excited atrial fibrillation | Irregular broad QRS with varying width |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Atrial fibrillation | Irregularly irregular rhythm with no P waves on 12-lead ECG [1] |
| Rapid ventricular response | Ventricular rate above 110 beats/minute [1] |
| Unstable | Shock, syncope, ischaemia or severe heart failure [2] |
| Stroke risk | CHA2DS2-VASc score (congestive heart failure, hypertension, age, diabetes, stroke, vascular disease, sex) [1] |
### Bedside and bloods
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12-lead ECG | Sinus rhythm | Irregularly irregular, no P waves | Confirms diagnosis | [1] |
| Potassium, magnesium | Potassium 3.5–5.0; magnesium 0.7–1.0 mmol/L | Low | Replace | [2] |
| Thyroid function | Normal | Suppressed thyroid-stimulating hormone | Treat thyrotoxicosis | [1] |
| Urea, creatinine, liver function | Creatinine 60–110 µmol/L | Raised | Adjust digoxin, enoxaparin and DOAC doses | [1] |
| Troponin, full blood count, glucose | Troponin below 99th percentile | Raised | Look for ischaemia or anaemia | [1] |
### Imaging and specialist
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Echocardiography | Ejection fraction 55% or above | Ejection fraction below 40% | Chooses rate-control drug | [2] |
| Cardiology | Not needed | Recurrent AF, structural disease or unstable | Rhythm control strategy | [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Monitor, oxygen only if saturation below 94%, intravenous (IV) access, ECG, bloods. [2]
- Unstable: emergency synchronised cardioversion. [1] [2]
- Treat the precipitant (sepsis, hypovolaemia, pain, alcohol, thyroid). [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Stable: rate control, or rhythm control if onset is under 48 hours. [1]
- Onset over 48 hours or unknown: rate control and anticoagulate; do not cardiovert until anticoagulated for 3 weeks or echo-guided. [1]
- Start anticoagulation by CHA2DS2-VASc: heparin at presentation, then a direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC). [1]
### Escalation
- Failure of rate control or deterioration: cardiology and critical care review. [2]
### Disposition
- Discharge when rate is controlled, stable and anticoagulation plan is set; admit for unstable, heart failure or unresolved cause. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NICE NG196 [1] | RCUK tachyarrhythmia V3 2026 [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Unstable | Emergency electrical cardioversion | Synchronised shock, up to 3 attempts |
| Onset under 48 hours | Rate or rhythm control | Rate or rhythm control |
| Rate control | Beta-blocker or calcium channel blocker first | Ejection fraction above 40%: beta-blocker, verapamil, diltiazem or digoxin; below 40%: beta-blocker or digoxin |
| Anticoagulation | Heparin at presentation; DOAC first; CHA2DS2-VASc | Anticoagulate if over 24 hours |
### Discharge criteria
- Resting rate below 110 beats/minute, stable blood pressure, anticoagulation started and follow-up arranged. [1]
### Follow-up
- Cardiology and anticoagulation review within 2 weeks; echocardiogram if not done. [1]
### Patient education
- Take the anticoagulant every day; report bleeding, black stool or stroke symptoms.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Reduce alcohol and caffeine; lose weight; exercise regularly.
### Team referrals
- Cardiology; anticoagulation clinic. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Choose rate control, rhythm control or shock, and start anticoagulation safely.
### Key points
- Unstable AF needs synchronised shock. [1] [2]
- Avoid verapamil and beta-blocker in decompensated heart failure. [2]
- Anticoagulate by CHA2DS2-VASc. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Cardioverting AF of unknown or over 48 hours onset without anticoagulation. [1]
- Giving a beta-blocker and verapamil together. [3]
- Missing pre-excitation. [2]
