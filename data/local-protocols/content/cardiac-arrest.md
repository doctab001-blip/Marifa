# Adult Cardiac Arrest (Advanced Life Support)

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) advanced life support for in-hospital or arriving cardiac arrest, based on the Resuscitation Council UK (RCUK) and European Resuscitation Council (ERC) 2025 Adult Advanced Life Support guidelines, the RCUK 2021 algorithm, the American Heart Association (AHA) 2025 Cardiac Arrest algorithm and the RCUK 2025 post-resuscitation care page, with doses from UK summaries of product characteristics. Traumatic arrest, pregnancy, hypothermia, drowning, anaphylaxis, children and decisions not to attempt resuscitation are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Cardiac arrest is loss of effective circulation; the rhythm is shockable (ventricular fibrillation or pulseless ventricular tachycardia) or non-shockable (asystole or pulseless electrical activity). [1]
- Survival depends on immediate high-quality chest compressions, early defibrillation for shockable rhythms and treatment of reversible causes. [1] [2]
- Brain injury begins within minutes, so time to first compression and shock decides outcome. [1]
- Reversible causes: the 4 Hs (hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypo- or hyperkalaemia and metabolic, hypo- or hyperthermia) and 4 Ts (thrombosis, tension pneumothorax, tamponade, toxins). [4]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Collapse; the patient cannot report symptoms. Witnesses may report chest pain, breathlessness or palpitations beforehand. [1]
### Examination signs
- Unresponsive, not breathing normally (agonal gasps do not count), no palpable carotid pulse within 10 seconds. [1]
### Red flags
- Shockable rhythm: deliver a shock at once. [1]
- Reversible cause evident (anaphylaxis, hypoxia, massive haemorrhage, tension pneumothorax). [4]
- Opiate, calcium channel blocker or beta-blocker poisoning. [4]
### Precipitants
- Acute coronary syndrome, arrhythmia, hypoxia, pulmonary embolism, electrolyte disturbance, poisoning, sepsis. [1] [4]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Syncope | Pulse present and breathing normally |
| Seizure | Pulse present; movements precede unconsciousness |
| Profound hypotension (pseudo-pulseless electrical activity) | Echocardiography shows cardiac activity and arterial line shows a pressure |
| Agonal breathing | Gasping with no effective circulation (still arrest) |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Cardiac arrest | Unresponsive, not breathing normally, no pulse [1] |
| Shockable | Ventricular fibrillation or pulseless ventricular tachycardia on the monitor [1] |
| Non-shockable | Asystole or pulseless electrical activity [1] |
### Bedside and bloods
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cardiac monitor or defibrillator pads | Sinus rhythm | Ventricular fibrillation, asystole or organised rhythm without pulse | Chooses the pathway | [1] |
| Capnography | End-tidal carbon dioxide 4–6 kPa | Below 1.3 kPa after 20 minutes of resuscitation suggests poor prognosis; sudden rise suggests return of circulation | Confirm airway and compression quality | [2] |
| Blood gas, potassium, glucose, lactate | Potassium 3.5–5.0 mmol/L | Potassium raised or low, glucose low | Treat reversible causes | [4] |
| Point-of-care ultrasound | Pump function present | Tamponade, right heart strain, no cardiac activity | Treat cause; limit pauses to 10 seconds | [1] |

| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-resuscitation 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) | Normal | ST elevation | Urgent coronary angiography | [7] |
| Cardiology and critical care | Not needed | Return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) | Post-resuscitation care | [7] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Call the resuscitation team; start cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) with chest compressions at 100–120/minute and 5–6 cm depth, ratio 30 compressions to 2 breaths until airway secured. [4]
- Attach the defibrillator and analyse the rhythm; shockable: shock immediately. [1]
- Intravenous (IV) or intraosseous (IO) access; give adrenaline by rhythm (see Medicines). [1]
- Treat reversible causes while resuscitating. [4]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Rhythm check every 2 minutes with minimal pause; swap compressors every 2 minutes. [4]
- Consider an advanced airway and capnography. [2]
- Pulmonary embolism as the cause: thrombolysis, then continue CPR for 60–90 minutes. [1]
### Escalation
- Refractory arrest: consider extracorporeal CPR in a suitable centre. [2]
### Disposition
- After ROSC: critical care or catheter laboratory. [7]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | RCUK/ERC 2025 [1] [2] | AHA 2025 [3] |
|---|---|---|
| Defibrillation | First shock at least 150 J biphasic | Manufacturer dose |
| Adrenaline | 1 mg every 3–5 minutes; after 3rd shock if shockable | 1 mg every 3–5 minutes |
| Amiodarone | 300 mg after 3 shocks, 150 mg after 5 | 300 mg then 150 mg |
| Lidocaine | 100 mg then 50 mg | 1–1.5 mg/kg then 0.5–0.75 mg/kg |
| Routine calcium, bicarbonate, steroids | Not recommended | Not recommended |
| Post-ROSC | Oxygen saturation 94–98%; mean arterial pressure above 60–65 mmHg; prevent fever 72 hours | Similar |
### Discharge criteria
- Not applicable; all post-arrest patients are admitted to critical care. [7]
### Follow-up
- Cardiology, neurology and rehabilitation; family support. [7]
### Patient education
- Explain the event, the cause found and the follow-up plan to the patient and family.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Cardiac rehabilitation and risk factor control after discharge.
### Team referrals
- Critical care; cardiology; neurology for prognosis. [7]
### Learning objectives
- Run the shockable and non-shockable pathways and treat reversible causes.
### Key points
- Compress hard and fast and shock early. [1]
- Adrenaline 1 mg early for non-shockable rhythms. [1]
- Do not give routine calcium or bicarbonate. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Long pauses in compressions. [4]
- Missing pulmonary embolism or tension pneumothorax. [4]
- Hyperventilation after return of circulation. [7]
