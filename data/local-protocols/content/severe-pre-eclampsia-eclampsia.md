# Severe Pre-eclampsia and Eclampsia

SCOPE: Emergency Department (ED) initial management of severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia in pregnant women (over 20 weeks of gestation or postpartum), covering blood pressure control and magnesium sulfate, based on the NHS Borders guideline "Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia" (last reviewed 1 March 2021). Delivery planning, HELLP syndrome management and neonatal care are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Pre-eclampsia is a placental disorder causing widespread endothelial dysfunction, hypertension, proteinuria and organ injury. [1]
- Eclampsia is a seizure in a woman with pre-eclampsia and is a medical emergency. [1]
- Severe hypertension causes stroke, so urgent blood pressure control is needed. [1]
- Magnesium sulfate prevents and treats eclamptic seizures; excess causes respiratory depression and cardiac toxicity. [1]
- Delivery is the definitive treatment. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Severe headache, visual disturbance, epigastric or right upper quadrant pain, vomiting, swelling, seizure. [1]
### Examination signs
- Hypertension, hyperreflexia with clonus, papilloedema, oedema, epigastric tenderness, reduced urine output. [1]
### Red flags
- Diastolic blood pressure above 120 mmHg once, or above 110 mmHg on two occasions 4 hours apart. [1]
- Seizure or impending eclampsia (headache, visual disturbance, clonus). [1]
- Oliguria, abnormal liver enzymes, low platelets (HELLP syndrome: haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets). [1]
### Precipitants
- First pregnancy, multiple pregnancy, previous pre-eclampsia, chronic hypertension, diabetes, kidney disease, age over 40. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Epilepsy | Known seizure history without hypertension |
| Intracranial haemorrhage | Focal signs or very high blood pressure; seen on computed tomography |
| Acute fatty liver of pregnancy | Hypoglycaemia and liver failure |
| Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura | Neurological signs with haemolysis and no hypertension |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Severe hypertension | Diastolic above 120 mmHg once, or above 110 mmHg on two occasions 4 hours apart [1] |
| Blood pressure treatment goal | Diastolic blood pressure below 100 mmHg [1] |
| Magnesium toxicity | Level above 4.0 mmol/L, absent patellar reflexes or respiratory rate below 12/minute [1] |
### Bedside and bloods
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blood pressure, urine protein (dipstick or protein:creatinine ratio) | Below 140/90 mmHg; protein below 30 mg/mmol | Above 160/110 mmHg, protein 30 mg/mmol or more | Severe pre-eclampsia | [1] |
| Full blood count, liver function, urea and creatinine, uric acid | Platelets 150–400 × 10⁹/L; ALT below 40 IU/L; creatinine below 90 µmol/L | Platelets below 100, raised ALT, raised creatinine | HELLP syndrome, organ injury | [1] |
| Clotting and group and save | INR below 1.2 | Abnormal | Coagulopathy; epidural contraindicated | [1] |
| Magnesium level (if infusion continues) | 0.7–1.0 mmol/L | Above 4.0 mmol/L | Toxicity; stop infusion | [1] |
### Imaging and specialist
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cardiotocography and obstetric ultrasound | Normal fetal heart rate | Abnormal | Fetal compromise; delivery planning | [1] |
| Obstetric, anaesthetic and neonatal teams | Not needed | Severe disease or eclampsia | Call immediately | [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Eclampsia: call for help, left lateral tilt, protect airway, oxygen 15 L/minute, intravenous (IV) access, give magnesium sulfate. [1]
- Severe hypertension: start antihypertensive treatment at once and aim for diastolic pressure below 100 mmHg. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Hourly respiratory rate, patellar reflexes, blood pressure and urine output during magnesium; restrict fluids to 80 mL/hour total. [1]
- Avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. [1]
### Escalation
- Recurrent seizure: further magnesium bolus; unresponsive hypertension: add second agent and critical care. [1]
### Disposition
- Delivery suite with obstetric, anaesthetic and neonatal teams; magnesium continues for 24 hours after the last seizure or delivery. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NHS Borders guideline (2021) [1] | Standard UK practice (NICE NG133, not opened) [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Blood pressure goal | Diastolic below 100 mmHg | Below 135/85 mmHg after severe episode |
| First-line agent | Labetalol 50 mg IV then infusion; hydralazine alternative; nifedipine third | Labetalol, nifedipine or hydralazine |
| Magnesium | 4 g load, 1 g/hour for 24 hours | 4 g load, 1 g/hour for 24 hours |
### Discharge criteria
- Not applicable in the ED: severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia are admitted to the delivery suite for delivery planning and postnatal observation. [1]
### Follow-up
- Postnatal blood pressure review within 1 week; long-term cardiovascular risk review; counselling for future pregnancy (low-dose aspirin from 12 weeks). [2]
### Patient education
- Report headache, visual change, epigastric pain or reduced fetal movement; avoid NSAIDs.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Healthy weight, salt moderation and regular exercise between pregnancies.
### Team referrals
- Obstetrics, anaesthetics, neonatology, critical care. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Control severe hypertension and give magnesium sulfate with safe monitoring.
### Key points
- Diastolic below 100 mmHg is the goal. [1]
- Magnesium continues for 24 hours after the last fit. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Over-hydration causing pulmonary oedema. [1]
- Magnesium toxicity with oliguria. [1]
- Missing HELLP syndrome. [1]
