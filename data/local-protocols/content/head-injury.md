# Adult Head Injury

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) assessment of head injury, computed tomography (CT) selection, and initial medicine treatment. Recommendations were taken from a secondary summary of NICE NG232 (Head injury: assessment and early management, 2023); the NICE page itself could not be opened, so thresholds not in the summary are standard UK values and are flagged. Children and penetrating injury are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Head injury causes primary brain damage at impact and secondary damage from hypoxia, hypotension, raised intracranial pressure and bleeding. [1]
- Anticoagulant and antiplatelet drugs increase the risk of delayed intracranial haemorrhage. [1]
- Intracranial haematoma (extradural, subdural) expands and raises intracranial pressure. [1]
- Early tranexamic acid reduces head injury death when given within 3 hours. [2]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Headache, vomiting, loss of consciousness, amnesia, confusion, seizure. [1]
### Examination signs
- Reduced Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), focal neurological deficit, scalp haematoma or laceration, signs of skull base fracture (panda eyes, Battle sign, cerebrospinal fluid leak). [1]
### Red flags
- GCS 12 or below on initial assessment, or below 15 at 2 hours. [1]
- Suspected open, depressed or skull base fracture. [1]
- Post-traumatic seizure or focal deficit. [1]
- More than one vomit, age 65 or over with loss of consciousness or amnesia. [1]
- Anticoagulant or antiplatelet use (other than aspirin alone). [1]
### Precipitants
- Falls, road traffic collisions, assault, sport; anticoagulants, alcohol, frailty. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Intoxication | Alcohol on breath; improves over hours |
| Hypoglycaemia | Low capillary glucose |
| Stroke or seizure causing the fall | Deficit or postictal state without impact |
| Cervical spine injury | Neck pain, midline tenderness |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| CT head within 1 hour | GCS below 13 initially, GCS below 15 at 2 hours, suspected skull fracture, seizure, focal deficit, more than one vomit [3] |
| CT head within 8 hours | On anticoagulant or antiplatelet (not aspirin alone); presenting after 8 hours: scan within 1 hour [1] Rec. 1.5.13 |
| CT with age 65 or over | Loss of consciousness or amnesia; dangerous mechanism [3] |
| Tranexamic acid | GCS 12 or below [1] Rec. 1.3.17 |
| No admission | Isolated simple linear skull fracture, not anticoagulated [1] Rec. 1.9.1 |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Airway with cervical spine protection, oxygen, intravenous (IV) access, glucose, GCS and pupils. [3]
- Intubate if GCS 8 or below, loss of airway or seizures (see the Rapid Sequence Intubation protocol). [3]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Observe GCS, pupils, limb power and vital signs every 30 minutes until GCS 15, then hourly for 4 hours, then every 2 hours. [3]
- Give tranexamic acid if GCS 12 or below; treat pain; reverse anticoagulation if bleed found. [1]
### Escalation
- Deterioration, bleed on CT or GCS 8 or below: neurosurgery and critical care; raised pressure treatment. [1]
### Disposition
- Discharge with written advice and a responsible adult if CT normal (or not indicated) and GCS 15. [1] Rec. 1.9.1

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NICE NG232 (summary, 2023) [1] | Standard UK practice (NICE CG176 style, not opened) [3] |
|---|---|---|
| Anticoagulated patient | CT within 8 hours; within 1 hour if over 8 hours since injury | CT within 8 hours |
| Tranexamic acid | GCS 12 or below | 1 g over 10 minutes then 1 g over 8 hours within 3 hours |
| Skull fracture | Isolated simple linear fracture needs no admission if not anticoagulated | Same |
### Discharge criteria
- GCS 15, normal CT if indicated, tolerating oral intake, no worrying features, responsible adult at home for 24 hours. [1]
### Follow-up
- Return advice; general practitioner and concussion clinic if symptoms persist beyond 2 weeks. [1]
### Patient education
- Return for worsening headache, vomiting, drowsiness, weakness, fits or fluid from nose or ear.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Rest, avoid alcohol and contact sport until symptom-free; graded return to activity.
### Team referrals
- Neurosurgery; stroke or falls service for older adults; safeguarding if needed. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Select CT by risk and treat anticoagulated head injury promptly.
### Key points
- Anticoagulated patients need early CT. [1] Rec. 1.5.13
- Tranexamic acid within 3 hours if GCS 12 or below. [1] Rec. 1.3.17
### Pitfalls
- Attributing drowsiness to alcohol. [3]
- Missing delayed bleed on anticoagulants. [1]
- Discharging alone. [1]
