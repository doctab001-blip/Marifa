# Reversal of Anticoagulation (Warfarin and DOACs)

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) urgent reversal of warfarin and direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) for life-threatening bleeding or emergency surgery, based on the NHS Highland Anticoagulant Reversal Guidelines (TAM699 version 2, last reviewed 1 July 2025). Heparin and low-molecular-weight heparin reversal follow standard UK practice and are flagged. Non-bleeding supratherapeutic INR and restarting anticoagulation are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Anticoagulants prevent clot formation, so bleeding on treatment can be life-threatening and needs rapid reversal. [1]
- Warfarin reduces vitamin K-dependent clotting factors II, VII, IX and X; reversal needs vitamin K for sustained effect and prothrombin complex concentrate for immediate effect. [1]
- Dabigatran is a direct thrombin inhibitor reversed by the antibody fragment idarucizumab. [1]
- Apixaban and rivaroxaban are factor Xa inhibitors reversed by andexanet alfa; no specific agent exists for edoxaban. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Bleeding, headache, melaena, haematemesis, weakness, collapse. [1]
### Examination signs
- Shock, expanding haematoma, neurological deficit, bruising. [1]
### Red flags
- Intracranial haemorrhage. [1]
- Haemodynamic instability from bleeding. [1]
- Bleeding into a closed space or critical organ. [1]
- Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) or known hypersensitivity (contraindications to concentrate). [1]
### Precipitants
- Drug interactions, acute kidney injury (DOAC accumulation), excess dose, trauma. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Liver failure coagulopathy | Normal DOAC levels, raised bilirubin |
| Disseminated intravascular coagulation | Low platelets and low fibrinogen |
| Trauma-induced coagulopathy | Major injury without anticoagulant |
| Thrombocytopenia | Low platelet count |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Warfarin reversal | Life-threatening bleeding with INR above 1.5, or catastrophic bleeding with INR unknown [1] section Warfarin |
| Prothrombin complex dose band | INR 2.0–3.9: 25 units/kg; INR 4.0–6.0: 35 units/kg; INR above 6.0: 50 units/kg [1] section Warfarin |
| Prothrombin complex ceiling | Weight capped at 100 kg [1] section Warfarin |
| DOAC reversal | Life-threatening bleeding on a DOAC, with haematologist approval [1] section DOACs |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Stop the anticoagulant; resuscitate; direct pressure and local haemostasis; intravenous (IV) access; send coagulation bloods. [1]
- Warfarin: give vitamin K and prothrombin complex concentrate from the resuscitation fridge before haematology advice if bleeding is life-threatening. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Observations at 10 and 30 minutes after concentrate; repeat coagulation screen at 30 minutes; record the batch number; inform the Blood Transfusion Service; consider tranexamic acid. [1]
### Escalation
- Persistent bleeding after reversal: haematology and critical care; consider further concentrate and surgery. [1]
### Disposition
- Critical care or high-dependency admission; decision on restarting anticoagulation by the specialist team. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NHS Highland TAM699 (2025) [1] | Standard UK practice (not opened) [3] |
|---|---|---|
| Warfarin | Vitamin K 10 mg IV with prothrombin complex by INR band, capped at 100 kg | Vitamin K 5–10 mg IV with prothrombin complex 25–50 units/kg |
| Dabigatran | Idarucizumab 5 g IV | Idarucizumab 5 g IV |
| Apixaban or rivaroxaban | Andexanet alfa with haematologist approval | Andexanet alfa, or prothrombin complex if unavailable |
| Edoxaban | No specific agent; discuss with haematology | Prothrombin complex 50 units/kg |
### Discharge criteria
- Bleeding controlled and coagulation normalised; plan agreed for restarting anticoagulation; patient is never discharged directly from the ED after reversal. [1]
### Follow-up
- Haematology or anticoagulation clinic review within 1 week. [1]
### Patient education
- Report bleeding or black stools immediately; carry anticoagulant alert card.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Consistent vitamin K intake on warfarin; limit alcohol.
### Team referrals
- Haematology; blood transfusion service; critical care or neurosurgery as indicated. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Reverse warfarin by INR band and choose the DOAC reversal agent.
### Key points
- Give vitamin K and concentrate together in warfarin bleeds. [1]
- Record batch numbers and tell the blood transfusion service. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Waiting for the INR in catastrophic bleeding. [1]
- Giving concentrate in heparin-induced thrombocytopenia. [1]
- Forgetting a separate cannula for vitamin K. [1]
