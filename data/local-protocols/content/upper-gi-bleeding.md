# Acute Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding (Including Variceal)

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) resuscitation and first-hours treatment of acute upper gastrointestinal bleeding, including suspected variceal bleeding, based on the NHS Borders guideline "Upper gastro-intestinal (GI) haemorrhage" (last reviewed 1 November 2022) and the NHS Scotland AUGIB bundle (appendix 2), with warfarin and octreotide values from standard UK practice (British Society of Gastroenterology and National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) CG141, not opened). Lower gastrointestinal bleeding, pregnancy and anticoagulant reversal detail (see the Reversal of Anticoagulation protocol) are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Acute upper gastrointestinal bleeding is bleeding from the oesophagus, stomach or duodenum, most often from peptic ulcer, varices, erosions or Mallory-Weiss tears. [1]
- Blood loss causes hypovolaemia and anaemia; variceal bleeding in cirrhosis can be rapid and fatal. [1]
- Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, aspirin, anticoagulants, alcohol and Helicobacter pylori are common risks. [1]
- Early endoscopy finds and treats the source; transfusion is restrictive. [1] [2]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Haematemesis, coffee-ground vomit, melaena, light-headedness, syncope, epigastric pain. [1] [2]
### Examination signs
- Tachycardia, hypotension, pallor, black tarry stool on rectal examination; stigmata of chronic liver disease (jaundice, ascites, spider naevi). [1]
### Red flags
- Shock (systolic blood pressure below 100 mmHg, heart rate above 100 beats/minute). [1]
- Haematemesis with chronic liver disease (suspect varices). [1]
- Haemoglobin below 70 g/L or ongoing bleeding after resuscitation. [1]
- On anticoagulants. [1]
### Precipitants
- Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, aspirin, anticoagulants, alcohol, portal hypertension, previous ulcer. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Lower gastrointestinal bleed | Fresh red blood per rectum without haematemesis; normal urea |
| Epistaxis or swallowed blood | Source seen in nose or throat |
| Haemoptysis | Frothy bright red blood with cough |
| Gastritis without bleeding | No melaena or haemoglobin fall |
| Iron or bismuth stool | Dark stool, tests negative for blood |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Low-risk (outpatient) | Glasgow-Blatchford Score (GBS) 0 or 1 [1] [2] |
| Transfusion threshold | Haemoglobin below 70 g/L, or shock [1] |
| Target haemoglobin | 70–100 g/L; in cirrhosis also 70–100 g/L, avoid over-transfusion [1] |
### Bedside and bloods
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full blood count | Haemoglobin 130–170 g/L (men) | Haemoglobin below 70 g/L | Transfuse; repeat | [1] section Transfusion |
| Urea and creatinine | Urea 2.5–7.8 mmol/L | Urea high out of proportion to creatinine | Upper gastrointestinal source; renal function for dosing | [1] |
| Coagulation (international normalised ratio) | Below 1.2 | Prolonged | Reverse anticoagulant; cirrhosis | [1] |
| Liver function and group and save or cross-match | Normal | Liver disease | Variceal suspicion; blood ready | [1] |
| Electrocardiogram (ECG), lactate | Normal | Ischaemia, high lactate | Cardiac risk; severity | [1] |
### Imaging and specialist
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy | No bleeding source | Ulcer, varices | Therapy; within 24 hours; urgent for suspected varices | [1]; [2] |
| Computed tomography angiography or interventional radiology | No bleeding | Active bleeding | When endoscopy fails | [1] |
| Surgical review | Not needed | More than 4 units in 24 hours or 8 units in 48 hours | Consider surgery | [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Airway and breathing; large-bore intravenous (IV) access; send full blood count, coagulation, urea, creatinine, liver function and cross-match. [1]
- IV crystalloid first; transfuse if shocked or haemoglobin below 70 g/L. [1]
- Shock: activate the major haemorrhage protocol (4 units of blood and 2 units of fresh frozen plasma at once, with urgent haematology advice). [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Calculate the GBS; suspected varices: terlipressin and antibiotics now. [1] [2]
- Endoscopy within 24 hours; urgent for suspected varices or failed resuscitation. [1] [2]
### Escalation
- Failed resuscitation or ongoing variceal bleeding: critical care, urgent endoscopy and Sengstaken tube for airway-protected patients. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit GBS above 1; GBS 0 or 1 outpatient endoscopy; high-risk to monitored bed. [1] [2]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NHS Borders upper GI haemorrhage (2022) [1] | NHS Scotland AUGIB bundle [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Risk score | GBS; 0 or 1 outpatient | GBS; consider discharge if 0 or 1 |
| Transfusion | Hb below 70 g/L or shock; target 70–100 | Hb below 70 g/L; target 70–100 |
| Variceal drug | Terlipressin 2 mg every 6 hours | Terlipressin 2 mg four times daily |
| Antibiotic | Ceftriaxone 1 g daily | Antibiotics (agent not stated) |
| Endoscopy | Next-day; urgent if varices | Within 24 hours |
| PPI | Omeprazole 80 mg then 8 mg/hour for 72 hours only after endoscopic therapy | IV PPI only if high-risk ulcer at endoscopy |
### Discharge criteria
- GBS 0 or 1 with outpatient endoscopy arranged; otherwise after endoscopy and haemostasis with stable haemoglobin. [1] [2]
### Follow-up
- Gastroenterology; Helicobacter pylori testing; review of antithrombotics and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. [1]
### Patient education
- Report black stool, vomiting blood or dizziness; avoid anti-inflammatory painkillers and alcohol.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Stop smoking and alcohol; regular meals.
### Team referrals
- Gastroenterology and endoscopy; hepatology; critical care; haematology. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Resuscitate, risk score and treat varices and warfarin bleeding correctly.
### Key points
- Restrictive transfusion at haemoglobin 70 g/L. [1]
- Terlipressin and antibiotics for suspected varices. [1]
- Tranexamic acid is not given. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Over-transfusion in cirrhosis. [1]
- Giving a proton pump inhibitor infusion before endoscopy. [1]
- Missing anticoagulant use. [1]
