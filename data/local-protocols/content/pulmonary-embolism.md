# Pulmonary Embolism

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) diagnosis, risk stratification and first-hours treatment of acute pulmonary embolism (PE), based on the NHS Highland adult guideline TAM635 (version 2.1), the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) NG158 as summarised by PrescQIPP, and UK summaries of product characteristics. Pregnancy and children are outside this protocol (urgent obstetric advice is needed). No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Pulmonary embolism is blockage of the pulmonary arteries, usually by a clot from the deep veins of the leg. [1]
- The clot raises pulmonary artery pressure and strains the right ventricle, which can cause obstructive shock and cardiac arrest. [1]
- Dead space and ventilation-perfusion mismatch cause hypoxia and breathlessness. [1]
- Risk rises with surgery, immobility, cancer, previous venous thromboembolism, pregnancy and oestrogen therapy. [1] [4]
- Prognosis is set by haemodynamic state, right ventricular strain on imaging and troponin. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Sudden breathlessness, pleuritic chest pain, cough, haemoptysis, syncope; unilateral leg swelling. [1]
### Examination signs
- Tachypnoea, tachycardia, hypoxia, raised jugular venous pressure, loud second heart sound, calf swelling and tenderness. [1]
### Red flags
- Systolic blood pressure (SBP) below 100 mmHg or persistent hypotension (high-risk PE). [1]
- Syncope or cardiac arrest. [1]
- Oxygen saturation below 90%. [1]
- Right ventricular strain on imaging or raised troponin. [1]
### Precipitants
- Recent surgery or trauma, long-haul travel, immobility, active cancer, pregnancy, hormone therapy, previous clot. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Acute coronary syndrome | Ischaemic electrocardiogram (ECG) changes and troponin rise without clot on imaging |
| Pneumonia | Fever and consolidation on chest X-ray |
| Pneumothorax | Absent breath sounds and pleural line on imaging |
| Acute heart failure | Pulmonary oedema and raised natriuretic peptide |
| Aortic dissection | Tearing pain and unequal arm blood pressure |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Low or intermediate probability | Simplified Geneva score 0–2 (NHS Highland) [1] |
| Needs D-dimer | Simplified Geneva score 3 or above; use age-adjusted cut-off (age × 10 µg/L) over age 50 [1] |
| Confirmed PE | Filling defect on computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA) [1] |
| High-risk PE | Cardiac arrest, obstructive shock or persistent hypotension [1] |
| Low-risk PE | Simplified Pulmonary Embolism Severity Index (sPESI) score 0 with no right ventricular strain [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Resuscitate; oxygen to keep saturation 94–98%; monitor and obtain intravenous (IV) access. [1]
- High clinical probability: start anticoagulation before imaging if imaging is delayed. [4]
- Cardiac arrest or obstructive shock: thrombolysis after senior discussion. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Stable: start apixaban (see Medicines) or low molecular weight heparin if a direct oral anticoagulant is unsuitable. [1] [4]
- Assess right ventricular strain and sPESI, plus troponin, to set disposition. [1]
### Escalation
- Persistent hypotension or arrest: thrombolysis; consider catheter or surgical embolectomy. [1]
### Disposition
- High risk: monitored high-dependency bed; intermediate-high: medical admission, consider monitored bed; low risk (sPESI 0): early discharge with anticoagulation. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NHS Highland TAM635 [1] | NICE NG158 summary [4] | Apixaban SmPC [2] |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-line anticoagulant | Apixaban | Apixaban or rivaroxaban | 10 mg twice daily 7 days then 5 mg twice daily |
| Second-line | Rivaroxaban; LMWH if CrCl below 15 | LMWH then dabigatran or edoxaban; or LMWH with warfarin | Not applicable |
| Duration | 3 months provoked; 6 months unprovoked | Stop at 3 months if provoked; 3–6 months after cancer | At least 3 months |
| Thrombolysis | Alteplase 100 mg over 2 hours | Not covered | Not applicable |
### Discharge criteria
- Low-risk PE (sPESI 0), anticoagulant started and understood, no oxygen need, follow-up arranged. [1]
### Follow-up
- Anticoagulation review at 3 months; PE clinic if under 50, unprovoked, recurrent or intermediate-high or high risk. [1]
### Patient education
- Take the anticoagulant exactly as prescribed; report bleeding, new breathlessness or leg swelling.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Keep mobile, stay hydrated on long journeys, stop smoking; avoid new anti-inflammatory painkillers.
### Team referrals
- PE clinic; haematology for unprovoked or recurrent clot; oncology if cancer found. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Stratify PE risk, choose the right anticoagulant and recognise who needs thrombolysis.
### Key points
- Start anticoagulation early when probability is high. [4]
- Apixaban is first-line; adjust for renal function. [1] [2]
- Thrombolysis is for shock or arrest. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Using the guideline in pregnancy. [1]
- Missing a creatinine clearance below 15 mL/min before a direct oral anticoagulant. [1]
- Giving heparin and apixaban together. [1]
