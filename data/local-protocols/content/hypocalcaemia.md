# Hypocalcaemia (Acute)

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) and acute medical treatment of acute symptomatic hypocalcaemia, based on the Society for Endocrinology 2013 emergency guidance and a Scottish secondary care guideline (version 3.0, January 2025). Paediatric and chronic hypoparathyroidism care are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Hypocalcaemia is a low albumin-adjusted calcium. Symptoms usually start when adjusted calcium falls below about 1.9 mmol/L, depending on the speed of fall. [1]
- Common causes: thyroid or parathyroid surgery, hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency, malabsorption, renal impairment, bisphosphonates and low magnesium. [2]
- Low magnesium blocks parathyroid hormone release and action, so calcium often corrects when magnesium is replaced. [2]
- Low calcium increases neuromuscular excitability and prolongs the QT interval.

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Perioral and finger tingling, muscle cramps, carpopedal spasm, anxiety.
### Examination signs
- Chvostek and Trousseau signs, tetany, hyperreflexia.
- Seizure, laryngospasm, prolonged QT on the electrocardiogram (ECG).
### Red flags
- Adjusted calcium below 1.9 mmol/L or symptomatic at any level below normal. [1] [2]
- Seizure, tetany, laryngospasm or arrhythmia.
- Digoxin therapy or existing arrhythmia (continuous ECG monitoring during IV calcium). [1]
### Precipitants
- Recent thyroid or parathyroid surgery, malabsorption, renal impairment, bisphosphonate use, hypomagnesaemia. [2]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Artefact (EDTA contamination or drip-arm sample) | Resampling gives a normal result [2] |
| Hypoalbuminaemia | Normal adjusted calcium |
| Hyperventilation | Normal calcium; resolves with breathing control |
| Hypomagnesaemia | Low magnesium; calcium corrects after replacement [2] |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|

| Severe or symptomatic | Adjusted calcium below 1.9 mmol/L, or symptomatic below the reference range [1] [2] |
| Mild | 1.9–2.2 mmol/L and asymptomatic [2] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Confirm the result and exclude artefact. [2]
- Severe or symptomatic: this is a medical emergency; give intravenous calcium gluconate with ECG monitoring. [1]
- Never replace with calcium chloride 10%; it is not equivalent to calcium gluconate. [2]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Repeat the bolus until the patient is asymptomatic, then start an infusion if needed. [1] [2]
- Check magnesium and potassium and replace magnesium first or together. [2]
- Start oral calcium and, where indicated, alfacalcidol. [1] [2]
### Escalation
- Cardiac arrhythmia, digoxin, or ongoing symptoms: continuous ECG monitoring and senior review. [1]
- Low parathyroid hormone: endocrinology. [2]
### Disposition
- Admit severe or symptomatic hypocalcaemia; post-thyroidectomy patients with adjusted calcium above 2.1 mmol/L may be discharged with a recheck within one week. [1] [2]

### Discharge criteria
- Asymptomatic, adjusted calcium above 2.1 mmol/L on oral treatment, cause identified and follow-up arranged. [1] [2]
### Follow-up
- Calcium within one week of discharge, then at 1, 3 and 6 months if on active vitamin D; fortnightly until stable. [1] [2]
### Patient education
- Take calcium with food; report tingling, cramps or palpitations; recognise symptoms of too much calcium.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Adequate dietary calcium and vitamin D intake.
### Team referrals
- Endocrinology for low parathyroid hormone or persistent hypocalcaemia. [1] [2]
### Learning objectives
- Recognise symptomatic hypocalcaemia and give IV calcium gluconate safely.
- Replace magnesium and plan oral therapy and follow-up.
### Key points
- Give 10–20 mL of 10% calcium gluconate over 10 minutes with ECG monitoring. [1] [2]
- Replace magnesium. [2]
### Pitfalls
- Using calcium chloride 10% for routine replacement. [2]
- Missing artefact. [2]
- Over-treatment with active vitamin D causing hypercalcaemia. [1]
