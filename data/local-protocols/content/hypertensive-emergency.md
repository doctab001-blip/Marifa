# Hypertensive Emergency

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) management of hypertensive emergency (severe hypertension with acute target-organ damage), based on the MSD Manual Professional "Hypertensive Emergencies" (updated April 2026) and the Medycyna Praktyczna (empendium) drug table 3.9-2, with doses from UK summaries of product characteristics. Pregnancy and pre-eclampsia, acute stroke blood pressure targets and phaeochromocytoma crisis are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Hypertensive emergency is a severely raised blood pressure (usually above 180/120 mmHg) with acute damage to the brain, heart, kidney or eye. [1]
- Loss of autoregulation allows high pressure to injure small vessels. [1]
- Pressure that is lowered too fast can cause stroke, myocardial and renal ischaemia. [1]
- Severe hypertension without organ damage is urgency and is treated with oral medicines over days. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Headache, confusion, visual loss, chest pain, breathlessness, back pain (dissection), seizures, reduced urine. [1]
### Examination signs
- Blood pressure above 180/120 mmHg, papilloedema or retinal haemorrhage, focal neurology, pulmonary oedema, unequal arm blood pressure. [1]
### Red flags
- Chest or back pain with pressure difference between arms (aortic dissection). [1]
- Neurological deficit, seizure or encephalopathy. [1]
- Pulmonary oedema or acute coronary syndrome. [1]
- Acute kidney injury or haemolysis. [1]
### Precipitants
- Missed antihypertensives, stimulant drugs, renal disease, pregnancy, abrupt clonidine withdrawal, monoamine oxidase inhibitors. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Hypertensive urgency | No acute organ damage |
| Acute ischaemic stroke | Focal deficit; blood pressure is lowered only if thrombolysis planned |
| Phaeochromocytoma crisis | Paroxysms, sweating, palpitations |
| Pre-eclampsia | Pregnancy or postpartum |
| Pain or anxiety | Blood pressure falls when treated |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Severe hypertension | Blood pressure above 180/120 mmHg [1] |
| Hypertensive emergency | Severe hypertension plus acute damage to brain, heart, aorta, kidney or eye [1] |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blood pressure in both arms | Difference below 10 mmHg | Difference above 20 mmHg | Suspect dissection | [1] |
| 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) and troponin | Normal | Ischaemia, left ventricular hypertrophy | Acute coronary syndrome | [1] |
| Urea, creatinine, potassium | Creatinine 60–110 µmol/L | Raised | Kidney damage | [1] |
| Urinalysis | Negative for protein and blood | Protein, blood | Renal involvement | [1] |
| Full blood count and film | Normal | Fragmented red cells | Microangiopathy | [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Resuscitation area, continuous monitoring, intravenous (IV) access, arterial line if possible. [1]
- Identify the organ affected and choose the drug (see Medicines). [1]
- Do not give oral or sublingual nifedipine. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- General target: lower mean arterial pressure by 20–25% over the first 1–2 hours, then toward 160/100 mmHg over 2–6 hours. [1]
- Aortic dissection: systolic pressure 100–120 mmHg and heart rate below 60 beats/minute within 20 minutes. [1]
- Pulmonary oedema: use a nitrate with a loop diuretic. [1]
### Escalation
- Failure to control with one drug, or organ damage worsening: critical care and specialist review. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit to critical care or high-dependency unit for infusions. [1]

### Discharge criteria
- Not for ED discharge; step down to oral therapy and stable blood pressure on the ward. [1]
### Follow-up
- Hypertension clinic and secondary-cause screening within 1–2 weeks. [1]
### Patient education
- Take blood pressure medicines daily; avoid stimulant drugs; return for severe headache, vision change or chest pain.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Reduce salt, alcohol and weight; stop smoking.
### Team referrals
- Critical care; cardiology; nephrology; cardiothoracic surgery for dissection. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Separate emergency from urgency and choose a drug by organ damage.
### Key points
- Lower pressure by no more than 20–25% in the first hour. [1]
- Never give oral or sublingual nifedipine. [1]
- In dissection control heart rate first. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Over-rapid reduction causing ischaemia. [1]
- Treating asymptomatic severe hypertension as an emergency. [1]
- Vasodilator before beta-blocker in dissection. [1]
