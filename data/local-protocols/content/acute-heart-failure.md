# Acute Heart Failure and Pulmonary Oedema

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) recognition and first-hour treatment of acute heart failure with pulmonary oedema or cardiogenic shock, based on the Emergency Care BC "Acute Heart Failure Management" clinical summary (reviewed 17 August 2020) with doses from the UK summaries of product characteristics. Chronic heart failure optimisation, valve emergencies requiring surgery and acute coronary syndrome care are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Acute heart failure is a sudden or worsening failure of the heart to maintain output or to clear the lungs, causing fluid in the lungs and poor organ perfusion. [1]
- Causes: acute coronary syndrome, arrhythmia, hypertension, valve disease, myocarditis, medicine or diet non-adherence, renal failure. [1]
- Raised filling pressures push fluid into the lung air spaces and cause hypoxia. [1]
- Congestion (wet) with good perfusion is the commonest picture; cardiogenic shock (wet and cold) is the worst. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Breathlessness at rest or lying flat, orthopnoea, cough with frothy sputum, ankle swelling. [1]
### Examination signs
- Tachypnoea, crackles, raised jugular venous pressure, third heart sound, peripheral oedema, cold peripheries in shock. [1]
### Red flags
- Oxygen saturation below 90% despite oxygen. [1]
- Systolic blood pressure (SBP) below 90 mmHg or signs of shock. [1]
- Ongoing chest pain or ST elevation. [1]
- Sustained arrhythmia. [1]
### Precipitants
- Ischaemia, arrhythmia, infection, uncontrolled hypertension, missed medicines, kidney failure, anaemia, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease exacerbation | Wheeze and hyperinflation; normal heart size and brain natriuretic peptide |
| Pneumonia | Fever, focal consolidation |
| Pulmonary embolism | Pleuritic pain, clear lungs, raised D-dimer |
| Acute respiratory distress syndrome | Normal heart on echocardiography |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Acute heart failure | Breathlessness with signs of congestion and raised natriuretic peptide or echocardiographic dysfunction [1] |
| Cardiogenic shock | SBP below 90 mmHg with signs of hypoperfusion after filling [1] |
### Bedside and bloods
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen saturation | 94–98% | Below 90% | Oxygen to keep above 90%; consider continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) | [1] |
| 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) | Normal | Ischaemia, atrial fibrillation | Treat cause | [1] |
| Troponin | Below 99th percentile | Raised | Acute coronary syndrome pathway | [1] |
| B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) | Below 100 ng/L | Above 400 ng/L | Supports heart failure | [1] |
| Urea, creatinine, potassium, sodium | Creatinine 60–110 µmol/L | Raised | Adjust diuretic; watch potassium | [1] |
| Venous or arterial gas, lactate | Lactate below 2 mmol/L | Raised lactate | Hypoperfusion; shock | [1] |
### Imaging and specialist
| Test | Normal (adult) | In this condition | What it means / next step | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray | Clear lungs | Upper lobe diversion, interstitial oedema, effusions | Confirms congestion | [1] |
| Bedside echocardiography | Normal ejection fraction | Reduced ejection fraction, valve lesion, effusion | Guides inotropes; exclude tamponade | [1] |
| Cardiology and critical care | Not needed | Shock, ischaemia, refractory hypoxia | Urgent referral | [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Sit upright; oxygen to keep saturation above 90% (do not give routine oxygen if above 94%). [1]
- Intravenous (IV) access, monitoring, ECG, bloods. [1]
- Start IV furosemide and, if SBP is 100 mmHg or above, a nitrate infusion. [1]
- Non-invasive ventilation or CPAP 5–10 cmH2O if respiratory distress persists. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Assess urine output and respond to diuretic by 1–2 hours; repeat or double the furosemide dose if poor response. [1]
- Treat the precipitant (ischaemia, arrhythmia, infection). [1]
### Escalation
- SBP below 90 mmHg or hypoperfusion: inotrope or vasopressor and urgent critical care and cardiology. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit to a monitored bed; critical care for non-invasive ventilation or inotropes. [1]

### Discharge criteria
- Stable on oral diuretic, no oxygen requirement, weight at dry weight, precipitant treated and heart failure follow-up arranged. [1]
### Follow-up
- Heart failure clinic within 2 weeks; electrolytes and creatinine within 1 week. [1]
### Patient education
- Daily weights; return if weight rises 2 kg in 3 days or breathlessness worsens.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Limit salt and fluid as advised; stop smoking; take medicines every day.
### Team referrals
- Cardiology and heart failure nurse; critical care when needed. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Treat pulmonary oedema with diuretic and nitrate, and recognise cardiogenic shock needing inotropes.
### Key points
- Do not give GTN with SBP below 100 mmHg. [1] [3]
- Reassess urine output within 1–2 hours of diuretic. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Routine oxygen when saturation is normal. [1]
- Over-diuresis in kidney injury. [1]
- Missing an acute coronary syndrome cause. [1]
