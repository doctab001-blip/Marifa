# Thyroid Storm

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) recognition and drug treatment of thyroid storm (thyrotoxic crisis), based on the Norfolk and Norwich NHS trust guideline for suspected thyroid emergencies (version 3.2, 2022) and the MSD Manual treatment table. Definitive treatment and long-term care are outside this protocol. The American Thyroid Association guideline could not be opened. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Thyroid storm is a life-threatening exaggeration of thyrotoxicosis, usually in Graves disease, triggered by infection, surgery, trauma, iodine load or stopping antithyroid drugs. [1]
- Excess thyroid hormone causes fever, tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, heart failure, agitation, delirium, vomiting and liver dysfunction. [1]
- Heart rate control is the first priority because tachycardia, atrial fibrillation and heart failure cause most deaths. [1]
- Iodine is given after the thionamide because earlier iodine can feed hormone synthesis and worsen thyrotoxicosis. [1]
- Treatment has five parts: control heart rate (beta-blocker), block hormone synthesis (thionamide), block hormone release (iodine, given after the thionamide), reduce conversion and treat adrenal stress (steroid), and support (fluids, cooling, treat the precipitant). [1] [2]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Palpitations, sweating, heat intolerance, tremor, agitation, vomiting, diarrhoea, weight loss.
### Examination signs
- Fever above 38°C, tachycardia above 110 beats per minute (with or without atrial fibrillation), heart failure, agitation or delirium, goitre or eye signs. [1]
### Red flags
- Temperature above 38°C with tachycardia above 110. [1]
- Heart failure, atrial fibrillation or delirium. [1]
- Burch–Wartofsky score above 45, or 25–45 (impending storm, treat as storm). [1]
- Jaundice or liver dysfunction.
### Precipitants
- Infection, surgery, trauma, iodine or contrast load, stopping antithyroid medicines, labour, amiodarone. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Sepsis | Source of infection; normal thyroid function tests |
| Neuroleptic malignant syndrome | Antipsychotic exposure and rigidity |
| Serotonin syndrome | Clonus with serotonergic drug exposure |
| Phaeochromocytoma crisis | Paroxysmal hypertension; normal thyroid tests |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Cardinal features | Fever above 38°C, tachycardia above 110, heart failure, agitation [1] |
| Burch–Wartofsky score above 45 | Thyroid storm confirmed [1] |
| Score 25–45 | Impending storm; treat as storm [1] |
| Score below 25 | Storm excluded [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Recognise by the cardinal features and the Burch–Wartofsky score and start treatment without waiting for blood tests. [1]
- Give a beta-blocker (propranolol) unless contraindicated. [1] [2]
- Give a thionamide (propylthiouracil) and start iodine only 60 minutes after the first thionamide dose. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Give steroid (hydrocortisone), then iodine at 60 minutes, then cholestyramine. [1]
- Support: IV fluids and glucose, cooling, treat the precipitant and consider central venous monitoring to guide fluids. [1] [2]
- Check blood glucose every 4 hours. [1]
### Escalation
- Call endocrinology and the high dependency unit; sedation with chlorpromazine 50–100 mg IM if needed. [1]
- Avoid beta-blockers in asthma: use a calcium channel blocker if beta-blockade is contraindicated. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit to high dependency or intensive care. [1]
### Medicines
Give in this order: beta-blocker, thionamide, then iodine 60 minutes after the first thionamide dose, with steroid.

### Discharge criteria
- Not for ED discharge; transfer to high dependency or ward under endocrinology once stable. [1]
### Follow-up
- Endocrinology follow-up and definitive therapy (iodine-131 or surgery) after the crisis is controlled. [2]
### Patient education
- Take antithyroid medicines as prescribed and report fever, sore throat or jaundice.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Avoid iodine-rich supplements and report new symptoms early.
### Team referrals
- Endocrinology and high dependency or critical care. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Recognise thyroid storm with the score and give drugs in the right order.
### Key points
- Give iodine only 60 minutes after the first thionamide dose. [1]
- Propranolol is not contraindicated in thyrotoxic heart failure; avoid in asthma. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Giving iodine before the thionamide. [1]
- Delaying treatment for blood results. [1]
- Missing the precipitant. [1]
