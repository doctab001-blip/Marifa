# Sickle Cell Crisis (Acute Painful Episode)

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) management of an acute painful sickle cell crisis, based on the St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust guideline "ED Management of Sickle Cell Acute Painful Crisis" (June 2019, approved August 2019, referencing NICE CG143). Children, pregnancy (obstetric specialist registrar review) and exchange transfusion decisions are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Sickle haemoglobin polymerises when deoxygenated, so red cells sickle, block small vessels and cause tissue ischaemia and severe pain. [1]
- Triggers are infection, dehydration, cold, hypoxia, stress and pregnancy. [1]
- Acute chest syndrome (lung infarction or infection), stroke, splenic sequestration and priapism are life-threatening complications. [1]
- Infection risk is high because of functional hyposplenism, so fever needs prompt antibiotics. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Severe pain in the limbs, back, chest or abdomen; fever; breathlessness; headache; weakness. [1]
### Examination signs
- Fever, pallor, jaundice, dehydration, tenderness over affected bones, hypoxia, chest signs. [1]
### Red flags
- Respiratory symptoms, chest pain, fever or oxygen saturation 95% or below (possible chest crisis). [1]
- Pain uncontrolled after the first dose of opioid or complications present. [1]
- Neurological deficit, severe headache or priapism. [1]
- Pregnancy. [1]
### Precipitants
- Infection, dehydration, cold exposure, hypoxia, alcohol, stress. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Acute chest syndrome | New infiltrate on chest X-ray with fever or hypoxia |
| Osteomyelitis | Fever with focal bone tenderness, raised inflammatory markers |
| Acute abdomen | Peritonism, focal tenderness |
| Stroke | Focal deficit or severe headache |
| Splenic sequestration | Falling haemoglobin with enlarging spleen |

## Investigations
### Diagnostic criteria
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Analgesia timing | First analgesia within 30 minutes of arrival, reassess every 30 minutes [1] |
| Severe pain | Pain score 7 or above [1] |
| Chest crisis flag | Respiratory symptoms, fever, chest pain or oxygen saturation 95% or below [1] |
| Refer to haematology | Pain uncontrolled or any complication [1] |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Give analgesia within 30 minutes; check oxygen saturation and give oxygen if 95% or below; intravenous (IV) access if needed. [1]
- Warm, rehydrate orally, take history of usual pain pattern and usual analgesia. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Reassess pain score and sedation every 30 minutes; repeat opioid; antibiotics if febrile. [1]
### Escalation
- Pain uncontrolled, chest symptoms or neurological signs: haematology, critical care, patient-controlled analgesia. [1]
### Disposition
- Admit if pain uncontrolled, any complication, fever or new neurological signs; discharge if pain controlled on oral analgesia. [1]

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | St George's ED guideline (2019) [1] | Standard UK practice (NICE CG143, not opened) [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Time to analgesia | Within 30 minutes, reassess every 30 minutes | Within 30 minutes |
| Severe pain opioid | Morphine 10 mg subcutaneous (5 mg if opioid-naive) | Strong opioid, titrated, consider patient-controlled analgesia |
| Fever | Co-amoxiclav plus clarithromycin; levofloxacin if penicillin allergic | Broad-spectrum antibiotics |
### Discharge criteria
- Pain controlled on oral analgesia, no fever, saturation above 95%, no complications, follow-up arranged. [1]
### Follow-up
- Haematology or sickle cell service within 1 week. [1]
### Patient education
- Hydrate, keep warm, take regular analgesia and return for chest pain, fever, breathlessness or weakness.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Folic acid, vaccination, avoid cold and dehydration; do not smoke.
### Team referrals
- Haematology; sickle cell nurse specialist; critical care for chest crisis. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Treat pain within 30 minutes and recognise chest crisis.
### Key points
- Analgesia within 30 minutes, reassessed every 30 minutes. [1]
- Fever is an emergency in sickle cell disease. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Delaying opioids because of suspected drug-seeking. [2]
- Over-hydration causing chest crisis. [2]
- Missing the first signs of chest syndrome. [1]
