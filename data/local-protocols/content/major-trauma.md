# Major Trauma: Primary Survey and Resuscitation

SCOPE: Adult (over 18 years) Emergency Department (ED) primary survey and initial resuscitation of major trauma, based on NICE NG39 (major trauma: assessment and initial management) and the Highland major haemorrhage protocol for blood product dosing. The structured primary survey (Airway with spine protection, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure) follows standard Advanced Trauma Life Support practice. Paediatric, pregnancy-specific and burns care are outside this protocol. No Saudi national guideline was found.

## Pathophysiology
Major trauma kills early through airway loss, hypoxia from chest injury and uncontrolled bleeding. [1]
- Haemorrhage is the leading preventable cause of death, so control of bleeding takes priority over fluid volume. [1]
- Early trauma-induced coagulopathy worsens with hypothermia, acidosis and crystalloid dilution. [1]
- Traumatic brain injury and spinal injury are protected by oxygenation, blood pressure and in-line immobilisation. [1]

## Signs & Symptoms
### Symptoms
- Pain, breathlessness, thirst, confusion; mechanism of injury (high-speed collision, fall from over 3 metres, penetrating wound). [1]
### Examination signs
- Airway obstruction, tracheal deviation, absent breath sounds, tachycardia, hypotension, rigid abdomen, unstable pelvis, reduced Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), limb deformity. [1]
### Red flags
- Airway compromise or GCS 8 or less. [1]
- Systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg or heart rate above 120/minute. [1]
- Life-threatening external bleeding. [1]
- Suspected pelvic or spinal injury. [1]
### Precipitants
- Road traffic collision, fall, assault, firearm or blast injury, crush injury. [1]
### Differentials
| Condition | The one feature that separates it |
|---|---|
| Tension pneumothorax | Absent breath sounds with tracheal deviation and shock |
| Cardiac tamponade | Distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds |
| Neurogenic shock | Low blood pressure with bradycardia and a sensory level |
| Medical collapse causing the injury (for example acute coronary syndrome (ACS) or seizure) | Preceding arrhythmia, chest pain, seizure or hypoglycaemia |

## Management
### Immediate (0–15 minutes)
- Airway with in-line immobilisation of the cervical spine; give oxygen; consider rapid sequence induction if airway or GCS requires it (see the Rapid Sequence Intubation protocol). [1] Rec. 1.2.1, 1.8.1
- Stop catastrophic bleeding first: direct pressure, haemostatic dressing, tourniquet, pelvic binder. [1] Rec. 1.5.1–1.5.3
- Two large-bore intravenous (IV) cannulae or intraosseous access; send bloods. [1]
### Ongoing (15–60 minutes)
- Tranexamic acid as soon as possible; blood products rather than crystalloid. [1] Rec. 1.5.4, 1.5.18–1.5.24
- Keep warm, treat pain, secondary survey and early CT. [1]
### Escalation
- Continued shock: activate the Major Haemorrhage protocol; theatre or interventional radiology. [2]
### Disposition
- Theatre, interventional radiology, CT, or critical care at a major trauma centre. [1] Rec. 1.1.1

### Guideline comparison
| Parameter | NICE NG39 [1] | Highland major haemorrhage TAM566 (2023) [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Tranexamic acid | As soon as possible; not after 3 hours unless hyperfibrinolysis | 1 g over 10 minutes within 3 hours, then 1 g over 8 hours |
| Blood products | Plasma to red cells 1:1; crystalloid only if no blood | 4 red cells with 4 plasma when coagulopathy risk |
| Haemorrhage control | Direct pressure, tourniquet, pelvic binder | Not covered |
### Discharge criteria
- Not applicable to the initial resuscitation: all patients with major trauma are admitted to a major trauma centre. [1] Rec. 1.1.1
### Follow-up
- Trauma rehabilitation, orthopaedic, neurosurgical and psychological follow-up. [1]
### Patient education
- Explain injuries, surgery and the recovery plan; wound and pain advice.
### Lifestyle and diet
- Protein-rich diet for healing; stop smoking; safety measures (seatbelt, helmet).
### Team referrals
- Trauma team, surgery, orthopaedics, neurosurgery, critical care, rehabilitation. [1]
### Learning objectives
- Perform an ordered primary survey and control haemorrhage before volume replacement.
### Key points
- Stop the bleeding first; blood not crystalloid. [1]
- Protect the spine and airway together. [1]
### Pitfalls
- Over-resuscitating with fluid. [1]
- Missing a pelvic fracture. [1]
- Delaying transfer for imaging. [1]
